Key by hand. n = 97/900 um^2 = 0.1078 um^-2, n_ref = 127/10^4 um^2 = 0.0127 um^-2, ratio 8.486. Critical region of the trimerization transition: three-dimensional XY (six-fold anisotropy irrelevant), nu = 0.6717, z = 2 from the stem. Line defects: areal density proportional to xi_hat^-2 and so to the cooling rate to the power 2 nu/(1 + z nu) = 0.5733. Rate = 0.265 K/h x 8.486^1.7444 = 0.265 x 41.69 = 11.0 K/h, which is 11 K/h. Any density from 95 to 99 points in the frame gives the same rounded rate.

Most tempting wrong answer: 19 K/h, from mean-field exponents (nu = 1/2, z = 2, rate going as the density squared), which hold only outside the critical region. Ising exponents give 12 K/h; point-defect scaling (xi_hat^-3) gives 3.2 K/h.

Check program (Python, standard library):

nu, z = 0.6717, 2.0
ratio = (97 / 30.0**2) / (127 / 100.0**2)
print(0.265 * ratio ** ((1 + z * nu) / (2 * nu)))   # 11.05
print(0.265 * ratio ** 2.0)                         # 19.09, mean-field
