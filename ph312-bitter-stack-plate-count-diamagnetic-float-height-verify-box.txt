The answer is not taken from a paper. It follows from the givens and from the number of copper plates in the photograph, so the photograph's content is listed here as text.

What the photograph shows: a central strip of the outer face of the stack, 78 thin dark insulating lines across bright copper, evenly spaced about 9.2 px apart, a darker steel flange at the top and at the bottom, and a dark tie-rod groove running down the face. Centres of the plates on a vertical line through the middle, in px from the top edge: 1st 37, 10th 119, 20th 210, 30th 302, 40th 394, 50th 486, 60th 578, 70th 670, 79th 752. The verification notes list all 79 as recovered from the image file.

The key by hand. 79 plates, each one turn of height H/79. A slit plate of uniform conductivity carries J = K/r, and I = K (H/N) ln(a2/a1) gives K = 25040 x 79/(0.1613 x ln 6.875) = 6.361e6 A/m. On the axis, with b = H/2, B(z) = (mu0 K/2)[F(a1) - F(a2)], F(a) = asinh((z+b)/a) - asinh((z-b)/a); the centre field is 13.12 T. The drop balances where -B dB/dz = mu0 rho g/|chi| = 1361.7 T^2/m. The product peaks at 1649 T^2/m, 5.4 mm below the top face; the upper root is the stable one, at 3.19 mm above the top face (B = 6.653 T, dB/dz = -204.7 T/m). Answer 3.2 mm.

Wrong answers that come up easily. 80 plates give 3.7 mm, the most tempting one, and 78 plates 2.6 mm. A uniform current density over the plate width gives no balance at all with 79 plates. The force written as |chi| B B'/(2 mu0) gives 14 mm.

A short check in Python (standard library):

import math
mu0 = 4e-7*math.pi
a1, a2, H, I, n = 0.01600, 0.1100, 0.1613, 25.04e3, 79
b = H/2
K = I*n/(H*math.log(a2/a1))
B = lambda z: mu0*K/2*sum(s*(math.asinh((z + b)/a) - math.asinh((z - b)/a)) for a, s in ((a1, 1), (a2, -1)))
dB = lambda z: mu0*K/2*sum(s*(1/math.hypot(a, z + b) - 1/math.hypot(a, z - b)) for a, s in ((a1, 1), (a2, -1)))
need = mu0*998*9.81/9.035e-6
lo, hi = b - 0.0054, b + 0.05
for i in range(60):
    m = (lo + hi)/2
    if -B(m)*dB(m) > need: lo = m
    else: hi = m
print(round(K), round(B(0), 3), round(need, 1), round((lo - b)*1000, 3))

It prints 6361279 13.116 1361.7 3.19.

Sources for the method: Berry and Geim, Eur. J. Phys. 18, 307 (1997); Simon and Geim, J. Appl. Phys. 87, 6200 (2000).
