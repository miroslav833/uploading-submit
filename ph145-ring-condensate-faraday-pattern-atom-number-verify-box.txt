The figure is the only source of the number of periods of the density wave. It is a simulated absorption image, 290 x 290 px, made for this question. The condensate is the bright ring of radius 110.5 px about the point (144.5, 144.5) px, and the density maxima are the brighter beads on it, 7.8 px apart along the ring. There are 89 of them, at the azimuths 1.5 degrees + k x 4.045 degrees for k = 0 to 88, counter-clockwise from the direction to the right (1.5, 5.5, 9.6, 13.6, 17.7, ...). By quadrant from the right, counter-clockwise, they number 22, 23, 22 and 22 (two of them, at 90.5 and 179.5 degrees, lie almost on the axes). Twenty spacings in a row span 80.9 degrees, which gives 20 x 360/80.9 = 89.0 without a full tally.

Key by hand. A standing density wave with m = 89 periods has k = m/R = 89/(140.0 um) = 6.357e5 per metre. Linearising the one-dimensional Gross-Pitaevskii equation about the uniform ring gives a Mathieu equation for the wave amplitude; the wave oscillates at half the modulation frequency and grows fastest when the modulation frequency is twice its Bogoliubov frequency, so f_B = 680.2/2 = 340.10 Hz. The kinetic energy of the wave is eps/h = hbar k^2/(4 pi M) = 291.106 Hz. From (h f_B)^2 = eps (eps + 2 g n): g n/h = (340.10^2 - 291.106^2)/(2 x 291.106) = 53.117 Hz. With g = 2 hbar omega_perp a and a = 6.53 a0 = 3.4555e-10 m the line density is n = 53.117/(2 x 4009 x 3.4555e-10) = 1.9172e7 per metre, and the ring holds 2 pi R n = 2 pi x 140.0e-6 x 1.9172e7 = 1.686e4 atoms (16,864 with unrounded intermediate values), which is 1.7e4.

The most tempting wrong answer is 2.7e4: 85 maxima in place of 89, which is where an estimate from the spacing lands (about 8 px judged by eye on a circumference of about 690 px gives 85 or 86; the spacing is 7.80 px). 85 maxima give 27,001 atoms and 86 give 2.4e4. Other routes with the right count: 2.1e5 (the wave taken to oscillate at the modulation frequency, f_B = 680.2 Hz, g n/h = 649.1 Hz), 6.3e4 (sound-wave limit, g n = M (omega/2k)^2), 3.4e4 (factor 2 missing in the Bogoliubov relation or in the coupling), 8.4e3 (g = 4 hbar omega_perp a), 2.4e5 (wavelength taken as twice the spacing of the maxima). Other miscounts: 87 maxima 2.2e4, 88 maxima 1.9e4, 90 maxima 1.4e4, 91 maxima 1.2e4, 94 maxima 5.0e3; with more than 96 maxima there is no solution.

Check program (Python 3, standard library):
import math
hbar, u, a0 = 1.05457e-34, 1.66054e-27, 5.29177e-11
M, R, f, fp, a, m = 7.016*u, 140.0e-6, 680.2, 4009.0, 6.53*a0, 89
eps = hbar*(m/R)**2/(4*math.pi*M)            # Hz
gn = ((f/2)**2 - eps**2)/(2*eps)             # Hz
print(eps, gn, 2*math.pi*R*gn/(2*fp*a))      # 291.106  53.117  16864

The verification notes add a Floquet calculation of the growth rates (only the wave with 89 periods grows at 680.2 Hz; 16,866 atoms), a direct integration of the Gross-Pitaevskii equation on the ring, and a script that counts the 89 maxima from the image file.
