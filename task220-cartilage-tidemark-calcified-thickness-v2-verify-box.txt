Hinges on reading the faint upper line as a reduplicated tidemark. The most superficial tidemark is the current mineralisation front, so calcified cartilage runs from it to the osteochondral junction (Lane and Bullough 1980; Burr 2004).

Image 2000 x 1500 px, 100 um bar = 335 px (3.35 px/um). Measured in 60-um vertical strips, lacunae and nuclei masked. Faint upper tidemark at a median depth of 150 um below the top of the field, bold lower tidemark at 213 um, osteochondral junction (irregular) at a mean of 322 um.

Matrix stain, green channel (lower = more basophilic): about 207 above the faint line, 192 between the lines, 172 below the bold one. The band between the lines already stains as calcified.

From the upper tidemark: 322 - 150 = 172 um (170.2 in the simulation), so 170 to the nearest 10 um.

Most tempting wrong answer: start at the bold tidemark, 322 - 213 = 110 um. Ordinary-looking for an adult, but it leaves out the 62 um band between the tidemarks.
