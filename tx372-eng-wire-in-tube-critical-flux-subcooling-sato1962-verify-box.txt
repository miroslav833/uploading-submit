Satō, Michiyoshi and Takeuchi 1962 (Bulletin of the JSME 5, 492-497), p. 495: Eq. (1) q = 4.68e3 dT_sat^1.71 (kcal/m2hr, C); Eq. (2) q_c/1e7 = 4.5 De^1.3 dt^0.824 (kcal/m2hr), De = tube diameter - heater diameter in m, dt = inlet subcooling in C. De = 0.00380 m, De^1.3 = 7.14034e-4, q_c = 32131.51 dt^0.824; dT_sat = 90.05 - dt. Equating: at dt = 66 the sides are 1.0763e6 and 1.0145e6, at 67 they are 1.0010e6 and 1.0271e6; root dt = 66.7001 K, dT_sat = 23.3499 K, q_c = 1.0233e6 kcal/m2hr. Eq. (2) as read follows the critical fluxes marked in the paper's figures within their scatter (2 mm tube at 80 C: 4.5e5 against 0.46-0.53e6; 4 mm at 50 C: 8.1e5 against 8.0e5); the exponent misread as 0.324 would be ten times too low. Most tempting wrong answers: 59 to 60 K from textbook boiling and critical-flux relations; 65.9 K with the bore itself for De.
