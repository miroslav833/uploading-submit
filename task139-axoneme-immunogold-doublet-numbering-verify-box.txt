The micrograph was made for this question, so the answer is checked from the figure itself. Numbering rule: doublet 1 of Chlamydomonas has no outer dynein arm and carries the 1-2 bridge (Hoops and Witman 1983), and the numbers run the way the arms point. The 14 cross-sections are taken row by row from the top left. For each one the data below give the direction of the arms in the picture (c clockwise, a anticlockwise) and, for every gold particle on it, the number of doublets counted clockwise in the picture from doublet 1 to the particle. By hand: a particle s steps clockwise is on doublet s + 1 in a clockwise section and on doublet 10 - s in an anticlockwise one. That gives 11 particles on doublet 3, 10 on doublet 7 and one each on doublets 2, 5 and 9, so the answer is 3 and 7. The most tempting wrong answer is 2 and 7: if the links are taken for a 5-6 bridge, the bridged doublet is called 5, every number goes up by 4 and 3 and 7 become 7 and 2. The figure shows the bridge on the doublet without outer arm, which is doublet 1. Numbering every section clockwise spreads the gold over doublets 3, 4, 7 and 8. Check in Python: d=[('c',[2,6]),('a',[5,7]),('a',[3]),('c',[2,2,6]),('a',[3,7]),('c',[6]),('c',[2,8]),('a',[3,3,7]),('c',[]),('a',[3,7]),('a',[7]),('c',[1,6]),('a',[3,7]),('c',[2])]; t=[0]*10; u=[0]*10
for v,g in d:
    for s in g: n=s+1 if v=='c' else (9-s)%9+1; t[n]+=1; u[(n+3)%9+1]+=1
print(t[1:], u[1:]) prints [0, 1, 11, 0, 1, 0, 10, 0, 1] and [1, 10, 0, 1, 0, 1, 11, 0, 1]. Notes: section 2 lists every cross-section, section 3 reads the figure back from image.png.
