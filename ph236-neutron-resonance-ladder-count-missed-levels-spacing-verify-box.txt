The answer is not taken from a paper or a data table; the nucleus is not a named one. It follows from the givens and from one count on the level diagram, so the content of the diagram is listed here as text.

What the diagram shows. The image is 866 x 160 px. The base line is the pixel row y = 128 and runs from x = 46 (0 keV) to x = 846 (13.05 keV). Each resonance found is a dark stick one pixel wide that rises from the base line; the shortest is 6 px long. There are 193 sticks. The key by hand. Uncorrected spacing 13050/193 = 67.62 eV. Spin-zero target, s waves: one channel, so x = Gn0/<Gn0> follows the chi-square law with one degree of freedom and the share above a limit x_t is erfc(sqrt(x_t/2)). The mean is <Gn0> = S0 D0, so x_t = 0.350e-3/(1.90e-4 D0) = 1.842 eV/D0 and 193 = (13050/D0) erfc(sqrt(0.9211/D0)). Iteration from 67.62 eV: 58.75, 58.11, 58.06, 58.06 eV. At the end x_t = 0.0317, missed share 0.1414, 224.8 levels in the interval, 31.8 missed. D0 = 58 eV. Counts of 192 and 194 sticks give 58.4 and 57.7 eV; 191 gives 58.7 and 195 gives 57.4.

Wrong answers that come up easily. 68 eV: no correction. 66 eV: an exponential width law (two degrees of freedom) in place of the one-channel law. 54 eV: erfc(sqrt(x_t)) with the factor 1/2 left out. 59 eV: one pass only, with the limit referred to the uncorrected mean width (58.75 eV), or the missed share added to the found number instead of dividing by the found share, or 189 to 191 sticks counted. 57 eV: 195 to 197 sticks. 60 and 61 eV: 184 to 188 sticks, close pairs taken as single sticks.

Check (Python, standard library):
import math
n = 193.0
for _ in range(60):
    n = 193/math.erfc(math.sqrt(0.350e-3*n/(2*1.90e-4*13050)))
print(13050/n)    # 58.06

A second check in the notes draws 600 level sequences with a true spacing of 58.0 eV (levels from random real symmetric matrices, widths from the one-channel law, limit 0.350 meV applied): on average 192.8 resonances survive and the estimate above returns 58.2 eV.

Sources for the method: Porter and Thomas, Phys. Rev. 104, 483 (1956); Fuketa and Harvey, Nucl. Instrum. Methods 33, 107 (1965); Lynn, The Theory of Neutron Resonance Reactions (1968); Mughabghab, Atlas of Neutron Resonances (2006).
