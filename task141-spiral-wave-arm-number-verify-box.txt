The answer cannot be looked up. Tomchik and Devreotes (1981) and Siegert and Weijer (1989) describe the spiral waves of cAMP and of cell movement round an aggregation centre and how period, spacing and speed are measured in dark-field recordings. Vasiev, Siegert and Weijer (1997) describe spirals with several arms in Dictyostelium. The value comes from the figure and from one line of kinematics: a pattern with N arms that turns once in T brings a front to a fixed cell every T/N.

Key by hand. The front at 234 px on the right-hand ray, followed anticlockwise, is the front at 280 px on the upward ray, at 318 px on the left, at 352 px on the downward ray and at 387 px back on the right. The right-hand ray has the fronts at 284 and 332 px between 234 and 387, so one turn moves a front out by three fronts: three arms. One turn takes 360/30 = 12 min, and 12/3 = 4 min. The front at 332 px goes through 368, 412, 432 to 479 px, again with two fronts (387, 439) in between.

The most tempting wrong answer is 12. It takes the pattern for a single spiral, one front per turn. A single spiral would move a front out by one spacing per turn, 11 px per quarter turn, where the figure shows 34 to 46 px. Two arms give 6 min, four arms 3 min.

Check with Python (standard library):
right = [114, 156, 196, 234, 284, 332, 387, 439, 479, 522, 561, 599]
for start, end in ((234, 387), (332, 479)):
    n = right.index(end) - right.index(start)
    print(start, end, n, 360 / 30 / n)
print([b - a for a, b in zip([234, 280, 318, 352], [280, 318, 352, 387])])
It prints 234 387 3 4.0, then 332 479 3 4.0, then the quarter-turn steps [46, 38, 34, 35].
