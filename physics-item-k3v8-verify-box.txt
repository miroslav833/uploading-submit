The figure is a simulated STM image (600 x 600 px). What is read from it is the angle between the dashed line (the mean step direction) and the rows of surface atoms. The dashed line runs from (184, 599) px to (599, 252) px, 39.9 degrees above the image horizontal. The atoms form a square lattice of 8.6 px whose rows run at 61.0 and 151.0 degrees; for example the atoms at (18, 392) px and (227, 16) px lie on one row of the upper terrace, 50 spacings apart. So the mean step direction makes 21.1 degrees with the nearest row. A principal-axis fit of the dashed line and a Fourier transform of the lattice give 21.11 degrees.

Key by hand. A step that never turns back is a staircase of single-atom moves along the two rows; with nearest-neighbour bonds every order has the same energy, so the moves are in random order (kinks of any depth). A fraction p = sin/(sin + cos) = 0.278 of the moves crosses the rows. Normal displacement variance per move a^2 sin cos; advance per move a/(sin + cos). Diffusivity b^2 = k_B T/stiffness = a sin cos (sin + cos) = 0.2556 x 0.3600 x 0.9330 x 1.2930 = 0.1110 nm. With attachment-detachment kinetics (mode decay rate Gamma_a stiffness q^2/k_B T) the mean square change at a fixed point is G(t) = (2/sqrt(pi)) sqrt(Gamma_a t b^2) = 1.128 x sqrt(8.50 x 3.00 x 0.1110) = 1.90 nm^2, so 1.9. Angles from 20.1 to 22.3 degrees give 1.9.

The most tempting wrong answer is 2.4 nm^2: the rows taken along the image axes, 39.9 degrees. Other values: rows along the image diagonals (5.1 degrees) 0.89; b^2 = a tan(theta) 1.8; only kinks one atom deep, b^2 = a sin cos (cos - sin), 1.3; the factor 2/sqrt(pi) left out, or b^2 = a sin cos per move, 1.7; deep kinks without the projection, b^2 = a tan (1 + tan), 2.1; next-nearest-neighbour rows taken as the rows (23.9 degrees) 2.0; b^2 doubled 2.7. Guesses without the figure: (2, 1) at 26.6 degrees 2.1, (3, 1) at 18.4 degrees 1.8, 45 degrees 2.4.

Check in a few seconds (Python 3, standard library). The first number is the key, the second the most tempting wrong answer:
import math
def G(deg):
    s, c = math.sin(math.radians(deg)), math.cos(math.radians(deg))
    return 2/math.sqrt(math.pi)*math.sqrt(8.50*3.00*0.2556*s*c*(s + c))
print(G(21.1))   # 1.898
print(G(39.9))   # 2.398
