Everything here turns on which line bounds the youngest bone structural unit. A unit is bounded by a reversal line, which is scalloped and cuts across older lamellae, while an arrest line is smooth and lies inside a unit (Parfitt et al. 1987). An osteon with a second reversal line inside it is a type II osteon (Ericksen 1991).

The image is 2400 x 2400 px and 300 um across, and the 50 um scale bar is 400 px. The canal centre is at about x 1185, y 1215 px. I measured these distances from the canal centre in the image file.
The canal surface is at a mean of 20.2 um (18.5 to 22.1).
The bold bright smooth ring is at a mean of 45.9 um, and the mean gray barely changes across it (+0.011, which is just the ring's own edge).
The faint scalloped line is at a mean of 73.6 um by ridge tracking, and the mean gray rises most steeply (+0.026) at 69.8 um. Its scallops are 11 to 20 um wide and 2.5 to 4.5 um deep, and the lamellae outside it are centred about 7 um off the canal and end obliquely against it.
The outer scalloped boundary against interstitial bone is at a mean of 121.4 um.

The youngest unit runs from the canal to the faint scalloped line, so 73.6 - 20.2 = 53.4 um (52.7 in the simulation). Measured to the scallop junctions it's 70 - 20.2 = 49.8, and to the crests 74.5 - 20.2 = 54.3. Every one of these is 50 um to the nearest 10 um.

The most tempting wrong answer takes the bold bright ring as the cement line, which gives 45.9 - 20.2 = 25.7 um and rounds to 30. Taking the whole osteon gives 121.4 - 20.2 = 101.2 um, or 100.

A quick check in standard-library Python
canal, arrest, reversal, outer = 20.2, 45.9, 73.6, 121.4
for r in (reversal, 70.0, 74.5, arrest, outer):
    print(r, round(r - canal, 1), int(10 * round((r - canal) / 10)))
prints 73.6 53.4 50, 70.0 49.8 50, 74.5 54.3 50, 45.9 25.7 30 and 121.4 101.2 100.
