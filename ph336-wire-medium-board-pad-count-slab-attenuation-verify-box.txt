The answer is not taken from a paper. It follows from the givens and from the number of wires in the photograph, so the photograph's content is listed here as text.

What the photograph shows: a long laminate board seen from straight above on a dark bench, carrying a grid of small bright copper pads, 6 rows of 71, evenly spaced about 8.9 px apart in both directions; the board is turned by under one degree. Columns of the pads in the first row, in px from the left edge: 1st 48, 10th 128, 20th 217, 30th 306, 40th 395, 50th 484, 60th 573, 71st 671. The verification notes list all 426 pads as recovered from the image file.

The key by hand. a = 788.6/70 = 11.266 mm, ln(a/r) = 3.3381, k_p = sqrt(2 pi/3.3381)/a = 121.78 per m (5.811 GHz). k = 100.601 per m. kappa = sqrt(k_p^2 - k^2) = 68.633 per m. D = 6a = 67.59 mm, kappa D = 4.6392. T = 1/[cosh^2(kappa D) + ((kappa^2 - k^2)/(2 k kappa))^2 sinh^2(kappa D)] with (kappa^2 - k^2)/(2 k kappa) = -0.3918, so 1/T = 2676.5 + 0.1535 x 2675.5 = 3087.2, which is 34.9 dB. Answer 35 dB.

Wrong answers that come up easily. 72 wires, or the period taken as 788.6/71, give 36 dB, the most tempting one, and 70 wires give 33.5 dB. The bare factor exp(-2 kappa D) gives 40 dB.

A short check in Python (standard library):

import math
c = 299792458.0
n, m, W, r, f = 71, 6, 0.7886, 0.400e-3, 4.800e9
a = W/(n - 1)
kp = math.sqrt(2*math.pi/math.log(a/r))/a
k = 2*math.pi*f/c
K = math.sqrt(kp*kp - k*k)
x = K*m*a
T = 1/(math.cosh(x)**2 + ((K*K - k*k)/(2*k*K))**2*math.sinh(x)**2)
print(round(a*1000, 3), round(kp, 2), round(K, 3), round(x, 4), round(-10*math.log10(T), 2))

It prints 11.266 121.78 68.633 4.6392 34.9.

Sources for the method: Pendry, Holden, Stewart and Youngs, Phys. Rev. Lett. 76, 4773 (1996); Belov et al., Phys. Rev. B 67, 113103 (2003).
