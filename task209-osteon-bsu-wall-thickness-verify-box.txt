The answer depends on which line bounds the youngest bone structural unit. A unit is bounded by a reversal line (scalloped, cutting across older lamellae); an arrest line is smooth and lies inside a unit (Parfitt et al. 1987). An osteon with a second reversal line inside it is a type II osteon (Ericksen 1991).

Image: 2400 x 2400 px, 300 um across, scale bar 50 um = 400 px. Canal centre at about x 1185, y 1215 px. Distances from the canal centre, read from the image file:
canal surface, mean 20.2 um (18.5 to 22.1)
bold bright smooth ring, mean 45.9 um, no change in mean gray across it (+0.011, the ring's own edge)
faint scalloped line, mean 73.6 um by ridge tracking, 69.8 um where the mean gray rises most steeply (+0.026), scallops 11 to 20 um wide and 2.5 to 4.5 um deep; the lamellae outside it are centred about 7 um off the canal and end obliquely at it
outer scalloped boundary against interstitial bone, mean 121.4 um

Key: youngest unit = canal to the faint scalloped line. 73.6 - 20.2 = 53.4 um (52.7 in the simulation); to the scallop junctions 70 - 20.2 = 49.8, to the crests 74.5 - 20.2 = 54.3. All give 50 um to the nearest 10 um.

The most tempting wrong answer takes the bold bright ring as the cement line: 45.9 - 20.2 = 25.7 um, which rounds to 30. Taking the whole osteon gives 121.4 - 20.2 = 101.2 um, or 100.

Python check (standard library):
canal, arrest, reversal, outer = 20.2, 45.9, 73.6, 121.4
for r in (reversal, 70.0, 74.5, arrest, outer):
    print(r, round(r - canal, 1), int(10 * round((r - canal) / 10)))
It prints 73.6 53.4 50, 70.0 49.8 50, 74.5 54.3 50, 45.9 25.7 30, 121.4 101.2 100.
