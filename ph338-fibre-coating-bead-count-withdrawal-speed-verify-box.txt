The bead count is only in the picture. Following the fibre from the bead under A (image position about (44, 235)) to the bead under B (946, 260) there are 98 beads, so 97 spacings of 9.31 px on average (single spacings about 7.7 to 11.1 px). Every tenth bead from A (image x): 10: 141, 20: 231, 30: 327, 40: 425, 50: 514, 60: 609, 70: 698, 80: 789, 90: 880, and B (97): 946.

By hand: lambda = 74.79 mm/97 = 771.03 um. Thin-film growth rate proportional to k^2 (1/R^2 - k^2), R = b + h, largest at k^2 = 1/(2 R^2), so lambda = 2 pi sqrt2 (b + h): b + h = 771.03/8.8858 = 86.772 um, h = 24.272 um, h/b = 0.38834. Ca = (0.38834/1.34)^(3/2) = 0.15602, U = gamma Ca/eta = 0.02060 x 0.15602/0.0485 m/s = 66.27 mm/s, which is 66 mm/s.

The most tempting wrong answer is about 259 mm/s, from the neutral wavelength 2 pi (b + h) in place of the fastest-growing one. 96 or 98 spacings give 70 or 63 mm/s.

Check in Python (prints 66.27): import math; h=74.79e3/97/(2*math.pi*math.sqrt(2))-62.50; print(round(20.60e-3/0.0485*(h/(1.34*62.50))**1.5*1e3, 2))
