Key: 31.1 W K-1 of overall thermal conductance. Section 2 of the attached notes prints it on the line "conductance U A 31.0996 W/K", and Section 3 reaches it again on the line "INDEPENDENT RESULT: 31.1 W/K", by a route that carries the flow as a mass flux, builds the Nusselt number in logarithms and forms the conductance from the inner surface. Section 4 holds the correlation, the geometry, the properties and the validity window as transcribed from the article. By hand: each of the seven tubes carries 1.48/(60000 x 7) = 3.5238e-06 m3/s, which through a 5.15 mm bore of area 2.0831e-05 m2 is 0.16916 m/s. Then Re = 1032 x 0.16916 x 0.00515/0.00083 = 1083.2 and Pr = 3895 x 0.00083/0.409 = 7.9043, and the article's correlation gives Nu = 2.43 x 1083.2^0.30 x 7.9043^0.094 = 24.012. Its Nusselt number is built on the overall coefficient referred to the outer tube surface, so Uo = 24.012 x 0.409/0.00635 = 1547 W/(m2 K) over an outer area of 7 pi x 0.00635 x 0.144 = 0.020109 m2, and the conductance is 31.100 W/K. With the convention the question states, the diameter common to the coefficient and the area cancels, UA = Nu k N pi L, so referring both to the inner diameter gives 1907 W/(m2 K) over 0.016309 m2 and the same 31.100 W/K; pairing a coefficient on the inner diameter with the outer area instead would give 38.4 W/K, which is listed among the wrong answers. The tempting wrong answer is 16.2 W K-1, from the classical Sieder-Tate laminar relation, Nu = 1.86 (Re Pr Di/L)^(1/3) = 12.54, which is 48 per cent low because the laminar-transition data of this baffled seven-tube bundle rise far above it. Dittus-Boelter gives 14.8. Putting either coefficient on the inner diameter while keeping the outer area, an inconsistency that raises both by the diameter ratio, still leaves 20.0 and 18.3, which is why the question names the outer diameter as the characteristic length. The fully developed laminar value of 3.66 gives 4.74, and passing the whole flow through one tube gives 55.8 with a Reynolds number of 7583, outside the window the correlation states. Check program: import math; Re = 1032*((1.48/60000/7)/(math.pi*0.00515**2/4))*0.00515/0.00083; Pr = 3895*0.00083/0.409; print(7*math.pi*0.144*0.409*2.43*Re**0.30*Pr**0.094) prints 31.0996. Citations: Hassaan and others, Scientific Reports 16, 22687 (2026), for the correlation, its Nusselt definition, its validity window, the test-section geometry and the fluid properties; Sieder and Tate, Industrial and Engineering Chemistry 28, 1429 (1936), for the classical relation it is measured against.
