Key: HLA-B*56:01:01:05S, accession HLA18158 in IPD-IMGT/HLA, a name assigned in February 2018. Section 2 of the attached notes prints it on the line beginning KEY: and section 3 reaches it by striking out each expression suffix the findings contradict; section 4 holds the copied rows with the release, the download date and the SHA-256 of both listing files.

By hand: the antibodies recover a heavy chain from the supernatant, so the gene is expressed, which excludes a null allele; staining of the donor's cells is at background rather than low, which excludes the low expression suffix; the chain leaves the cell, which excludes the cytoplasmic suffix; the trait follows the haplotype, which excludes the aberrant and questionable suffixes. What is left is the suffix for a secreted molecule. Exon 5 encodes the transmembrane segment, and the substitution at the intron 4 acceptor site costs the transcript that exon, so the chain has no anchor. The substitution lies in an intron, so the difference is recorded in the fourth field. Release 3.66.0 holds two alleles with the secreted suffix, one in the B*44:02 group and one in the B*56:01 group; the sequence of exons 2 and 3 fixes the group, which gives the name above.

Most tempting wrong answer: HLA-B*56:01:01:02S, the right group with the fourth field carried over from HLA-B*44:02:01:02S, the secreted allele that comes to mind first. The release gives B*56:01:01:02 to an allele with no suffix, so that string names nothing. HLA-B*44:02:01:02S itself is the answer of a reader who does not check the group; HLA-B*56:01:01:01 is the common allele of the group, read off exons 2 and 3 alone; and HLA-B*56:01:01:05 without the suffix is not a name the release holds.

Check in Python against the two listing files (prints B*56:01:01:05S): import re; print([n for n in (l.split(',')[1].strip() for l in open('Allelelist.txt') if ',' in l) if n.startswith('B*56') and n.endswith('S')])

Sources: Balas et al., HLA 92, 250 (2018) for the intron 4 splice site mutation and the loss of exon 5; Marsh et al., Tissue Antigens 75, 291 (2010) and HLA 107, e70595 (2026) for the fields of a name and the letters for unusual expression; Barker et al., Nucleic Acids Res. 54, D1152 (2026) for the database. The suffix on this allele is the assignment the release itself carries; the 2018 report describes the splice mutation and does not use the word secreted.
