The answer is not in a paper. It follows from the number of skyrmions and antiskyrmions inside the central square of the cross, which only the figure shows, and from the topological Hall effect of the citations above. The uploaded notes contain the scripts and their output.

What the figure shows. A 320 x 320 px image; vacuum in the four corners, the cross arms 200 px wide, central square from 60 to 260 px in x and y. Light-core ellipses (long axis horizontal) and dark-core ellipses (long axis vertical) are Bloch skyrmions of the two handednesses; small squares with two light and two dark sides are antiskyrmions. Inside the square: 30 antiskyrmions, 22 light-core and 21 dark-core skyrmions. In the arms: 46 antiskyrmions, 17 light-core and 20 dark-core skyrmions. Key by hand. Skyrmions of both handednesses have the same charge (the helicity does not enter Q), antiskyrmions the opposite one, so |Q| = |43 - 30| = 13. <B_em> = 13 (h/e)/w^2 = 13 x 4.1357e-15/4.840e-12 = 1.1108e-2 T. rho_THE = P R0 <B_em> = 0.410 x 2.45e-10 x 1.1108e-2 = 1.116e-12 ohm m. V = rho_THE I/t = 1.116e-12 x 0.600e-3/160.0e-9 = 4.18e-9 V. Answer 4.2 nV.

Most tempting wrong answer. The net charge over the whole frame, |80 - 76| = 4, gives 1.3 nV. A net charge of 12 or 14 gives 3.9 or 4.5 nV. Handedness taken as the sign of the skyrmion charge gives 9.3 or 10 nV; h/2e in place of h/e gives 2.1 nV.

Check program (Python 3, standard library; prints 13, 0.011108 T, 1.1158e-12 ohm m, 4.184 nV):

q = abs(30 - (22 + 21))
phi = 6.62607015e-34 / 1.602176634e-19
b = q * phi / (2.200e-6) ** 2
rho = 0.410 * 2.45e-10 * b
V = rho * 0.600e-3 / 160.0e-9
print(q, b, rho, V * 1e9)
