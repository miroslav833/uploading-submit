Key depends on what the 154 pA contains. The second EPSC starts on the tail of the first, and the program measures both peaks from the baseline before stimulus 1.

From the image file (bars 258 px = 50 pA, 252 px = 10 ms): EPSC1 98 pA. Black trace at the second peak 150 pA below baseline, 4.5 ms after stimulus 2. Grey single-stimulus trace at that same time 32 pA below baseline. Model behind the figure: tail 33.8 pA, EPSC2 alone 119.7 pA.

With the stated numbers: (154 - 34)/100 = 1.20. Key 1.2.

Most tempting wrong answer: 154/100 = 1.5. Second one: EPSC2 measured from the level at stimulus 2 (44.6 pA), (154 - 44.6)/100 = 1.09, so 1.1. The tail keeps decaying under EPSC2, so the right subtraction is the single-pulse trace at the time of the peak. A typical AMPA decay (8 to 10 ms) guessed without the traces gives 1.4.

Check, standard-library Python
for tail in (33.8, 44.6, 0): print(round((154 - tail)/100, 2))
Output: 1.2 1.09 1.54

Rounding: 1.2 for any tail from 30 to 38 pA (1.16 to 1.24).

Sources: Chiu and Carter, Front. Cell. Neurosci. 16, 916626 (2022), Methods, Analysis; Zucker and Regehr, Annu. Rev. Physiol. 64, 355-405 (2002), abstract p. 355; Debanne et al., J. Physiol. 491, 163-176 (1996), abstract p. 163.
