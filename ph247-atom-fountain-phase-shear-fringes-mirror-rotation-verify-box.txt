The map carries two things: the fringe period along x and the way the fringes lean. It is a simulated map of 920 x 790 px; the frame spans x = -7.6 to 7.6 mm and z = -6.4 to 6.4 mm at 50 px per mm (x = 0 at column 458, z = 0 at row 403), with camera pixels of 0.1 mm. The fringes are straight, parallel and of even contrast. Bright fringe centres on the line z = 0, read back from the image file: x = -6.36, -5.01, -3.65, -2.30, -0.95, 0.40, 1.76, 3.11, 4.46, 5.81, 7.16 mm. Ten periods span 13.52 mm, so the period along x is 1.352 mm (11.2 periods across the 15.2 mm). The period along z is 0.793 mm, and the fringes fall towards +x at 60 degrees from the vertical, so about 27 fringes cross the frame in all, 16 of them through its left and right sides.

Key by hand. k = 4 pi/780.241 nm = 1.6106e7 rad/m. Phase of the three pulses with a wave vector turning at Omega about the line of sight, last pulse late by dT: Phi = (k/t_d)(2 Omega T^2 x + dT z) for a point source imaged at t_d after release. 2 k T^2/t_d = 1.4048e7 rad s/m; 2 pi/1.352 mm = 4647 rad/m; Omega = 330.8 micro-rad/s. Lines of constant phase have slope -2 Omega T^2/dT; dT > 0 and the fringes fall towards +x, so Omega is clockwise for the camera (top of the vertical towards east). The Earth turns the laboratory about the north-pointing horizontal at 7.2921e-5 cos(34.20 deg) = 60.3 micro-rad/s, clockwise for a camera looking north (east side sinks), the same sense. Mirror in the laboratory: 330.8 - 60.3 = 270.5, that is 270 micro-rad/s. The answer stays 270 for a period from 1.334 to 1.375 mm, and for every given moved by half its last digit (270.1 to 270.9).

Other values: 230 for a period along x of 1.52 mm, ten periods across the map (the most tempting wrong answer; it comes from judging the slope and the spacing along z by eye instead of marking fringe centres along a horizontal line, which meets 11.2 periods); 330 with the Earth left out; 390 with the Earth's part added; 140 if the wave vector is taken to turn at twice the mirror rate; 220 with 2T as the expansion time; 260 with the full Earth rate and no cosine; 290 with the sine of the latitude; 60 for a mirror that only cancels the Earth; 320 and 350 for 13 and 14 periods across the map.

Check in a second (Python 3, standard library):
import math
lam, T, td, lat, OmE, period = 780.241e-9, 1.040, 2.480, 34.20, 7.2921e-5, 1.352e-3
A = 2*(4*math.pi/lam)*T*T/td
tot = 2*math.pi/period/A; earth = OmE*math.cos(math.radians(lat))
print(A, tot*1e6, earth*1e6, (tot - earth)*1e6)      # 1.4048e7 330.8 60.3 270.5
