The figure is the only source of the number of atoms. It is a simulated topograph, 372 x 520 px, made for this question, with the distortion of an uncorrected drift: the vertical scale changes smoothly between 0.73 and 1.21 from row to row, and the rows are shifted sideways by a smoothly changing amount of up to 14 px. The chain is a row of 62 separate bright protrusions in a shallow S from (38, 489) px at the lower left to (328, 30) px at the upper right; their spacing in the image varies between 7.6 and 11.1 px. Atoms no. 1, 11, 21, 31, 41, 51, 61 and 62, counted from the lower left end, sit at (38, 489), (72, 417), (129, 355), (183, 296), (244, 221), (281, 130), (322, 39) and (328, 30) px. By bands of image rows the chain has 8 atoms in rows 0 to 99, 11 in rows 100 to 199, 13 in rows 200 to 299, 17 in rows 300 to 399 and 13 below row 400. The length of the row divided by the largest and the smallest spacing, plus one, is 51 and 74, so a spacing measured at one place does not give the count; a count of the protrusions does. The eight dark spots are defects of the terrace, all well away from the chain.

Key by hand. With c_j = (a_j + i b_j)/2 the Hamiltonian is (i/2) sum a_j M_jk b_k, where M has -mu on the diagonal, -(t + Delta) above it and -(t - Delta) below it; the quasiparticle energies are the singular values of M. A vector with M v = 0 obeys (t - Delta) v_(j-1) + mu v_j + (t + Delta) v_(j+1) = 0, so v_j = r^j sin(j theta) with r = sqrt((t - Delta)/(t + Delta)) = 0.909112 and cos(theta) = -mu/(2 sqrt(t^2 - Delta^2)), theta = 1.751338 rad. It fails only in the last row, by (t + Delta) v_(N+1). Its mirror image sits at the other end, and the coupling of the two gives E = (t + Delta) v_1 |v_(N+1)| / sum v_j^2 = [Delta (4 t^2 - mu^2)/(t (t + Delta))] r^N |sin((N+1) theta)|/sin(theta). With N = 62: 167.972 micro-eV x 2.7181e-3 x 0.36955/0.98375 = 0.1715 micro-eV, which is 0.17 micro-eV. The exact smallest singular value is 0.17152 micro-eV.

The most tempting wrong answer is 0.26 micro-eV: the gap (93.5 micro-eV, or 2 Delta) times exp(-N Delta/t), the usual estimate without an oscillating factor. Other routes: 0.46 (the envelope of the closed form without |sin((N+1) theta)|), 0.15 (the closed form with theta taken as arccos(-mu/2t), the pairing left out of the wave number), 0.19 (r^N replaced by exp(-N Delta/t) and the prefactor by 4 Delta sin^2(theta)), 0.34 (twice the energy, the distance between the two peaks of a spectrum), 0.46 (phase N theta in place of (N+1) theta), 0.43 (cosine in place of sine), 93 (the gap of the infinite chain, end modes ignored), 104 (the lowest excitation at fixed fermion parity, the sum of the two lowest quasiparticle energies). Among the estimates A r^L and A exp(-L Delta/t) with A = Delta, 2 Delta, 4 Delta, the gap, half the gap or twice the gap and L = 61, 62 or 63, the nearest is 16 per cent away; Delta/sin(theta) times exp(-61 Delta/t) is 14 per cent away. Forms for small Delta that keep the right count and the phase (N+1) theta are approximations of the result and lie between 0.17 and 0.19 (for example 4 Delta sin(theta) r^(N+1) |sin((N+1) theta)| = 0.171). Miscounts: 61 atoms 0.50, 63 atoms 0.36, 60 atoms 0.010, 64 atoms 0.26, 59 atoms 0.61, 65 atoms 0.21 micro-eV. A count estimated from a spacing read at one place can be far off; 86 atoms, for which (N+1) theta happens to lie within 0.1 degree of a maximum of the oscillating factor, would give 0.047 micro-eV. Among all counts from 20 to 130 no other number of atoms comes closer than 15 per cent (72 atoms, 0.15 micro-eV).

Check program (Python 3 with NumPy), the smallest singular value of M:
import numpy as np
t, D, mu, N = 500.00, 47.50, 178.75, 62
M = -mu*np.eye(N) - (t + D)*np.eye(N, k=1) - (t - D)*np.eye(N, k=-1)
print(np.linalg.svd(M, compute_uv=False)[-1])   # 0.17152 (micro-eV)

The verification notes give the same number without a library, a Bogoliubov-de Gennes diagonalisation, a table over the atom counts 20 to 130 and a script that counts the 62 atoms from the image file.
