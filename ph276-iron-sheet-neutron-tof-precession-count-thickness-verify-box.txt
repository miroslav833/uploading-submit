The record carries one value, the number of precession turns between the arrows A (4.150 ms) and B (29.43 ms), both on polarization maxima (the uncalibrated spin turner fixes their phase, not t = 0). Each turn lasts 0.1708 ms (7.0 image pixels) and the trace grows noisy beyond about 14 ms; every tenth maximum after A falls near 5.858, 7.566, 9.274, 10.982, 12.691, 14.399, 16.107, 17.815, 19.523, 21.231, 22.939, 24.647, 26.355 and 28.064 ms, and B is the 148th: 148/25.28 ms = 5.854 turns per ms.

Key by hand. The spin turns by gamma_n x I x t/L (v = L/t), so I = 2 pi L N / (gamma_n (tB - tA)) = 18.00 m x 148 / (29.1647 MHz/T x 25.28 ms) = 3.6133 T mm. Neutrons sense B; the tangential gap field continues into the sheet, so the iron carries mu0(H + Ms) and I = 24.0 mT x 30.0 mm + 2.158 T x d = 0.7200 T mm + 2.158 T x d. d = 2.8933/2.158 = 1.3407 mm, that is 1.34 mm.

The most tempting wrong answer is 1.36 mm, from giving the iron only mu0 Ms and the 24.0 mT only to the rest of the gap. Leaving out the gap field gives 1.67 mm.

Check in a second (Python 3, standard library):
I = 18.00*148/(29.1647e6*(29.43e-3 - 4.150e-3))      # T m
print(I*1e3, (I - 0.0240*0.0300)/2.158*1e3)            # 3.6133 T mm, 1.3407 mm
