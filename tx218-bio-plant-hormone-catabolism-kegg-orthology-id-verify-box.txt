Key: K20624. It stands in section 2 of the attached notes (line Key) and again at the end of section 3, where it is reached through the gene locus. By hand: in the insertion line jasmonic acid, JA-Ile and 12-hydroxyjasmonic acid are unchanged, 12-hydroxy-JA-Ile rises from 0.55 to 1.62 and 12-carboxy-JA-Ile falls from 0.31 to 0.02 nmol per g, and the yeast-expressed enzyme oxidises 12-hydroxy-JA-Ile to the acid through the 12-oxo compound. That is CYP94C1 (At2g27690), EC 1.14.14.49 (Heitz et al., J. Biol. Chem. 287, 6296, 2012). In KEGG both ec:1.14.14.49 and ath:AT2G27690 link to K20624 (CYP94C1), whose gene list names At2g27690. Most tempting wrong answer: K20665 (CYP94B, EC 1.14.14.48), the first hydroxylation, excluded because its product accumulates. K20664, next to it, is an unrelated CYP716 entry. Check in Python on the stem's numbers: wt={'JA-Ile':0.42,'OH':0.55,'COOH':0.31}; mu={'JA-Ile':0.47,'OH':1.62,'COOH':0.02}; print({k: round(mu[k]/wt[k],2) for k in wt}) prints {'JA-Ile': 1.12, 'OH': 2.95, 'COOH': 0.06}: the substrate of the second oxidation piles up and its product is lost. Citations: Heitz et al., J. Biol. Chem. 287, 6296 (2012); Kanehisa et al., Nucleic Acids Res. 49, D545 (2021).
