Key: LMFA04040003. It stands in the attached notes in the output of the first script (line KEY) and in the last line of the output of the second. By hand: both hydroxyl oxygens come from $O_2$, so the soybean lipoxygenase acted twice. The first step gives the 17S hydroperoxide with a 13Z,15E diene; the second takes hydrogen from C-12 and adds $O_2$ at C-10 (S), which turns the 10Z bond into 11E. After reduction the acid is 10S,17S-dihydroxy-4Z,7Z,11E,13Z,15E,19Z-docosahexaenoic acid, an E,Z,E triene absorbing at 270 nm that inhibits platelet aggregation (Chen et al., FEBS Lett. 583, 3478 (2009)). The LIPID MAPS Structure Database lists it as Protectin DX under LMFA04040003. The most tempting wrong answer is LMFA04040001, neuroprotectin D1 (protectin D1), the famous 10,17-dihydroxy acid: it is 10R,17S with an 11E,13E,15Z triene and its C-10 oxygen comes from water through an epoxide (Serhan et al., J. Immunol. 176, 1848 (2006)), which the labelling excludes. LMFA04040002 is its 15-trans isomer and LMFA04040008 its 17R epimer. Check without the notes: open https://www.lipidmaps.org/rest/compound/lm_id/LMFA04040003/all and read sys_name; in Python, import json, urllib.request; print(json.load(urllib.request.urlopen('https://www.lipidmaps.org/rest/compound/lm_id/LMFA04040003/all'))['sys_name']) prints 10S,17S-dihydroxy-4Z,7Z,11E,13Z,15E,19Z-docosahexaenoic acid.
