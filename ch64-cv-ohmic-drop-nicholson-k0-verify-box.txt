Key depends on why the peak separation grows with disk size. Kinetics and planar diffusion don't depend on the disk; the uncompensated resistance of a disk with a distant reference is 1/(4 kappa r) (Newman 1966) and the peak current goes as r^2, so the ohmic shift goes as r.

Peaks (mV vs Fc+/Fc), cathodic / anodic / separation: 1.0 mm -1026 / -880 / 146; 2.0 mm -1033 / -875 / 159; 3.0 mm -1040 / -869 / 171; 5.0 mm -1053 / -859 / 194. Linear in r (0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.25 cm): intercept 134.4 mV. psi = 0.270, k0 = 0.270 x 0.02926 = 0.0079 cm/s, log10 k0 = -2.10. Any kinetic separation from 128.3 to 140.1 mV gives -2.1. Simulated with no ohmic drop: 132.7 mV.

Most tempting wrong answer: extrapolate against peak current (or area), 148.8 mV, -2.2. The 1.0 mm curve as it is, 146 mV, also -2.2.
