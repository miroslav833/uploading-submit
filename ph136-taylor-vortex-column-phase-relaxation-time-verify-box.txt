That is 182 lines, 5 to 7 px apart (the column of 1050 px over 183 vortices is 5.74 px each), so the column holds N = 183 vortices. The number must be odd: the plate at rest has an inward Ekman flow along it, the plate turning with the inner cylinder an outward one, so the two end boundaries are of different kinds, and with N vortices there are N + 1 boundaries of alternating kind.

Key by hand. k = pi N/L = 183 pi/170.50 = 3.37191/d, q = k - k_c = 0.23791/d. xi_0^2 q^2 = 0.071824 x 0.056603 = 0.0040655. Phase diffusion from the envelope equation: D = (xi_0^2/tau_0)(eps - 3 xi_0^2 q^2)/(eps - xi_0^2 q^2) = 1.89509 x 0.0051036/0.0132345 = 1.89509 x 0.38562 = 0.73079 nu. Phase fixed at both ends: slowest mode sin(pi z/L), tau = L^2/(pi^2 D) = 29070.25/(9.8696 x 0.73079) = 4030.5 d^2/nu. With d^2/nu = 25.0/2.50 = 10.0 s: tau = 40305 s = 11.2 h, that is 11 h.

The most tempting wrong answer is 8.6 h. It belongs to 182 vortices, the even neighbour that the rule for two plates at rest gives together with an estimate of the count: q = 0.21949/d, factor (eps - x)/(eps - 3x) = 2.0001, tau = 8.64 h. The other even neighbour, 184, gives 17.3 h; the odd neighbours 181 and 185 give 7.2 and 49.7 h; a pattern at the critical wavenumber (about 170 vortices) gives 4.3 h, as does the true count without the Eckhaus factor. More than 185 or fewer than 155 vortices lie outside the Eckhaus band. At 183 vortices: the factor eps/(eps - xi_0^2 q^2) alone on the time constant 5.6 h, the Eckhaus factor inverted 1.7 h, a slowest mode of wavelength L 2.8 h.

Check in a second (Python 3, standard library):
import math
kc, xi0, tau0, L, eps = 3.134, 0.268, 0.0379, 170.50, 0.0173   # units of d and d^2/nu
for N in (183, 182, 184, 181, 185):
    q = math.pi*N/L - kc
    D = xi0**2/tau0*(eps - 3*xi0**2*q**2)/(eps - xi0**2*q**2)
    print(L**2/(math.pi**2*D)*10.0/3600)                      # hours: 11.20, 8.64, 17.29, 7.24, 49.66
