What the figure carries. Ten vortices are marked with circles and letters. At A, C, D, F, H and J the extra fringe comes from above and ends at the centre of the circle (the fork opens upward). At B, E, G and I it comes from below. Away from the vortices the fringes are vertical, 6.2 px apart.

By hand. The bright fringes lie where theta - k x = 2 pi m. Above a counter-clockwise vortex the polar angle falls from pi to 0 from left to right, below it rises from -pi to 0, so a line above the core crosses one bright fringe more than a line below it. Fork open upward means counter-clockwise, sign +1. One vortex at r0 gives the cloud q hbar times the number of atoms outside r0, because the circulation round a circle about the axis is q h/m only if the circle encloses the core. For n0 (1 - r^2/R^2) that fraction is (1 - s^2)^2: 0.9341, 0.8148, 0.7136, 0.6099, 0.5308, 0.4450, 0.3577, 0.2774, 0.2020, 0.1262 for A to J. With the signs + - + + - + - + - + the sum is +1.201, which is +1.2.

The most tempting wrong answer is -1.2, from the fork rule the wrong way round. Without the weights the sum is +2; with the weight of a three-dimensional cloud, power 5/2, it is +1.1.

print(round(sum(qi*(1 - si*si)**2 for qi, si in zip(q, s)), 4))    # 1.201
