Key: BA 24:0;5bH;3aOH,7bOGlcNAc;24COOH

Where it stands: Liebisch et al., J. Lipid Res. 61, 1539-1555 (2020), open access, Table 6B, row "Ursodeoxycholic acid 7beta-N-acetylglucosaminide (UDCA-GlcNac)", column "Full Structure Level". The attached notes reproduce the whole table (first script, output line "Full structure level (key)").

By hand from the paper's rules: bile acid, class BA; cholan-24-oic acid has 24 carbons and no C=C, and the acid is written as a group, so 24:0; 5beta ring fusion, 5bH; ring oxygen functions with a/b, 3aOH and 7bOGlcNAc (sugar on a ring oxygen as in 3bOGlc for cholesteryl glucoside), joined by a comma as the row prints them, the semicolon being kept for a change of group type (testosterone: 17bOH;3oxo); free acid, 24COOH. Sibling rows built the same way: lithocholic acid BA 24:0;5bH;3aOH;24COOH, glycochenodeoxycholic acid BA 24:0;5bH;3aOH,7aOH;24COG.

Most tempting wrong answer: ST 24:1;O4;GlcNAc. It is the Species level entry of the same row (ST 24:1;O4;HexNAc: 24 carbons, one double-bond equivalent for the acid C=O, four oxygens, one N-acetylhexosamine) with the sugar named. It carries no positions and no stereochemistry, so it is not the Full structure level.

Check that the parts above give the printed string (Python 3, standard library):
ring = [(3, 'a', 'OH'), (7, 'b', 'OGlcNAc')]
print('BA 24:0;5bH;' + ','.join('%d%s%s' % g for g in ring) + ';24COOH')
