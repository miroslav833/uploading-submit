Key: 190 rpm (188.5 rpm before rounding). In the attached notes the key is the line "KEY n" of the section "Key, competing readings and half-last-digit table"; the closed-form CGS inversion of the section "Independent route" returns 188.52 rpm.

Source: Sano, Yamaguchi, Adachi, J. Chem. Eng. Japan 7, 255-261 (1974), free on J-STAGE. The abstract prints Sh = [2 + 0.4 (eps d_p^4/nu^3)^(1/4) Sc^(1/3)] phi_c with d_p the specific surface diameter and phi_c the surface factor; Eqs. (11) and (12) on p. 257 define d_p = 6/(rho_p S_w) and phi_c = 6/(rho_p S_w d_p'); Eq. (22) on p. 258 gives eps = N_p n^3 l^5/V.

By hand: d_p = 6/(1310 x 11.73) = 390.5 um, phi_c = 390.5/710 = 0.5500. Sh = 5.80e-5 x 3.9046e-4/0.78e-9 = 29.035; bracket = 29.035/0.55 = 52.795; Sc^(1/3) = 10.878; (eps d_p^4/nu^3)^(1/4) = 50.795/(0.4 x 10.878) = 11.674; eps = 0.8086 W/kg; V = 0.7854 m3; n = (0.8086 x 0.7854/(5.0 x 0.333^5))^(1/3) = 3.142 1/s = 188.5 rpm.

Most tempting wrong answer: 34 rpm, from writing Sh phi_c = bracket (dividing by the surface factor), 37 rpm with the screen size as d_p; it falls below the suspension speed of about 64 rpm (Zwietering). Surface factor left out: 81 rpm; spheres of the screen size: 85 rpm.
