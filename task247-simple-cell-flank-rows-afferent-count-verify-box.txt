Key depends on whether the blue flanks are the ON row's own surround. They aren't: they reach 0.78 of the peak and pooled surrounds stop at 0.375.

Map readings, peak = 1. Through the middle (0 on the horizontal axis): red band 0.99 on the midline, zero at 0.35 deg either side, blue minimum -0.78 at 0.67 deg (just past -0.8 near the ends of the band), zero again at 1.05 deg, then a pale red band up to 0.2 at 1.3 deg. Along the bands: red at half height at -1.99 and +1.99 deg, blue at half depth at -1.99 and +2.00 deg. Contours are drawn every 0.1, so the -0.7 contour inside each blue band and the 0.1 contour round each pale red band are plain to see.

Limit for one row. Across a dense row the profile is exp(-y^2/2s^2) - (r/k) exp(-y^2/2k^2s^2), with k >= 2 the width ratio and r <= 1 the volume ratio. Worst case k = 2, r = 1: with u = exp(-y^2/8s^2) it is u^4 - u/2, minimum -3/16 at u = 1/2, peak 1/2, ratio 0.375. k = 3 gives 0.294; k = 2 with r = 0.8 gives 0.232.

So the blue bands are OFF rows. The pale red bands are not rows: 0.2 against a blue depth of 0.78 is 0.26, under the limit, so the OFF rows' surrounds account for them. Two ON rows side by side and no OFF rows (32 afferents) fail twice: flanks reach 0.69 at most with a single top, and ON rows alone are never positive outside the flanks.

Three rows, OFF, ON, OFF. Half-height length 3.98 deg at 0.25 deg per afferent is 16 a row. 15 would end at 1.875 deg and 17 at 2.125 deg. 3 x 16 = 48.

Likely wrong answer: 16, one ON row with the flanks put down to its surrounds, as in Hubel and Wiesel's 1962 drawing. 80 if the pale red bands are taken for two more rows.
