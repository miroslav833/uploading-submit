Key rests on telling carcinoma cells from sinus histiocytes of the same tone and packing; only the nuclei differ. Size is the largest contiguous group of tumour cells (Steiner et al. 2018, histiocytes as the usual mimic).

Read from the pixels of the figure (1995 x 1295 px, bar 351 px = 0.5 mm, 702 px per mm):
tumour cells, marked by a dark nucleolus in a light nucleus: largest extent 1010 px = 1.44 mm
pale island (tumour plus histiocyte-filled sinus): largest extent 1705 px = 2.43 mm
mean grey of the two cell populations: 134 and 134, so tone alone doesn't separate them

Drawn: tumour cells about 16 um with 9 um vesicular nuclei and nucleoli; histiocytes about 17 um, foamy cytoplasm, 5 x 4 um dark oval nuclei, no nucleolus; lymphocytes 7 um.

Key: 1010/702 = 1.44, so 1.4 mm (micrometastasis).
Likely wrong answer: whole island 1705/702 = 2.43, so 2.4 mm (would read as a macrometastasis).
