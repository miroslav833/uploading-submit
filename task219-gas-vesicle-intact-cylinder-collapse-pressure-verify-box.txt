Measured from the pixels of the figure: the scale bar is 222 px = 100 nm (2.22 px per nm). The cylindrical part of each vesicle is 126.4 to 126.6 px wide, W = 57.0 nm, the same for the three. Stain exclusion across the cylinder, averaged along it, relative to the axis: 0.71 at 0.7 of the half-width and 0.49 at 0.88. A full cylinder excludes stain along its chord, sqrt(1 - x^2) = 0.71 and 0.48; a flattened two-wall ribbon would give 1.0 and 1.0. So the vesicles are intact and r = W/2 = 28.5 nm. p_c = E t^3 / [4 (1 - nu^2) r^3] = 3.4e9 x 8 / (3.64 x 28.5^3) Pa = 0.323 MPa, key 0.3 (widths of 56 to 61 nm give 0.34 to 0.26). The most tempting wrong answer is 1 MPa: the usual correction for a vesicle flattened on drying, r = W/pi = 18.1 nm, gives 1.251 MPa. Check in Python: W = 57.0; f = lambda r: 3.4e9 * 2.0**3 / (3.64 * r**3) / 1e6; print(round(f(W / 2), 3), round(f(W / 3.14159), 3)) prints 0.323 1.251. Where to look: the gray level across any vesicle, brightest on the axis and falling toward the edges. Section 2 of the notes (readback_png.py) makes these measurements from the PNG.
