Key: FYPO:0006692, the term cut with septum between unequally sized nuclei, defined in the release as an inviable phenotype of the vegetative growth phase in which a cell undergoes septation despite abnormal mitotic sister chromatid separation, producing inviable daughter cells, and in which the septum forms so as to separate the nucleus into two unequal masses, each in one compartment. Its curator comment adds that chromosome separation is entirely abolished in such cells. Section 2 of the attached notes prints it on the line beginning KEY: and section 3 reaches the same identifier by testing every candidate term against the observations one at a time; section 4 holds the copied rows with the release, the download date and the SHA-256 of the release file. Both sections read those copied rows, so they are two routes through one release and not two sources.

By hand: septation across chromatin that never divided, with inviable daughters, is the cut phenotype, FYPO:0000229. Cells longer than wild type at division exclude the subtypes that require early mitosis (FYPO:0003165 and the three terms under it, among them FYPO:0001054). Chromatin that never separated excludes FYPO:0001055, which requires normal condensation, and FYPO:0004592, which requires a normal division before the cut. What remains to be accounted for is that each compartment kept DNA and one kept more, which is the term above.

Most tempting wrong answer: FYPO:0000229, the general cut term, which a reader gives as soon as the phenotype is named. It covers the septum through the nucleus and leaves the unequal masses unrecorded. FYPO:0001054 matches the length of the cells, and its own synonym reads cut following delayed onset of mitosis, but its parent FYPO:0003165 requires entry into mitosis ahead of time. FYPO:0001917 matches the inviability, the length and the single septum, and is excluded because its parents FYPO:0002303 and FYPO:0007664 put the nucleus in only one compartment; FYPO:0007664 and FYPO:0007670 require a compartment with no DNA, which the stem denies. Both exclusions run through inherited claims rather than the terms' own definitions, which is the weak point of this item.

Check in Python against the release file (prints FYPO:0006692): import re; t=open('fypo.obo').read(); print([re.search(r'id: (FYPO:\d+)',s).group(1) for s in t.split('[Term]') if 'two unequal masses, each in one compartment' in s])
