Key by hand. Free length Lf = 315.9 - 98 x 2.93 = 28.76 um. Hard rods of length sigma between two walls move like points on the free length (subtract (i - 1/2) sigma from the i-th centre), and points that cannot pass behave as independent points with exchanged labels, so the slowest decay is that of one point with diffusion coefficient D0 and drift D0 F/kT between two reflecting walls: 1/tau = D0 [ (pi/Lf)^2 + (F/2kT)^2 ]. F = 0.127 pN x sin 0.115 deg = 2.549e-16 N, kT = 4.0729e-21 J, (F/2kT)^2 = 9.792e8 m^-2, (pi/Lf)^2 = 1.1932e10 m^-2, sum 1.2911e10 m^-2, times D0 = 5.85e-14 m^2/s gives 7.553e-4 s^-1, tau = 1324 s, which is 1300 s to two figures.

Most tempting wrong answer: 1400 s, from Lf^2/(pi^2 D0) without the drift term. 97 or 99 spheres give 1600 or 1100 s.

Check program (Python, standard library):

import math
kT = 1.380649e-23 * 295.0
Lf = 315.9e-6 - 98 * 2.93e-6
F = 0.127e-12 * math.sin(math.radians(0.115))
print(1 / (0.0585e-12 * ((math.pi / Lf) ** 2 + (F / (2 * kT)) ** 2)))   # 1323.9

The attached notes confirm the rate in three more ways: a diagonalisation of the one-particle operator, a diagonalisation of the full generator of extended rods on a lattice (its slowest rate equals that of one walker on the shortened row), and a direct Monte Carlo simulation of hard rods, which gives the predicted decay time within 3 per cent.
