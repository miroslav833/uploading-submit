Key: 0.96 m/s. The attached notes print it in the section 'Key, wrong routes and half-last-digit table' (line 'KEY: V = 0.9610 m/s -> 0.96 m/s') and in the independent route (root of the derivative, 0.96098 m/s). By hand, with Maruyama, Ando and Mizushina, J. Chem. Eng. Japan 13, 269 (1980): Eq. (9) on p. 272 gives dP/dx = dP_f [Fr_f/Fr + C0 K/(Fr sqrt(C_D))] with K = 15.6 (D/d)^0.3 (Eq. 8) and Fr_f = (2^n + Fr^n)^(1/n), n = 1.4 (Eq. 5, p. 270-271); Fr = V^2/(gD(s-1)) and C_D is the free-fall drag coefficient (Nomenclature, p. 274; Fig. 2 pairs glass beads of 2470 kg/m^3, 0.411 mm and 0.0635 m/s with C_D = 1.96). Here s = 2.6553, C_D = 1.3747, D/d = 57.14, K = 52.51, B = C0 K/sqrt(C_D) = 5.822, Fr = 1.5396 V^2. dP_f is proportional to V^1.75, so d ln G/d ln V = -0.25 + 2x(2^1.4 + x)^(1/1.4 - 1)/[(2^1.4 + x)^(1/1.4) + B] with x = Fr^1.4; it is -0.0179 at 0.93 m/s and +0.0171 at 0.99 m/s and vanishes at 0.9610 m/s, where the gradient is 1584 Pa/m. Every given moved by half its last digit keeps the root between 0.9596 and 0.9624 m/s. The most tempting wrong value is 1.5 m/s: a solver who does not have the paper uses the Durand form with K = 81 (1.46 m/s; K = 121 gives 1.67 m/s). Leaving out the net-section Froude number gives 0.74 m/s. Check program: from math import sqrt; g=9.81; D=0.04; d=7e-4; s=2650/998; Vt=0.105; CD=4*g*d*(s-1)/(3*Vt**2); B=0.13*15.6*(D/d)**0.3/sqrt(CD); a=1/(g*D*(s-1)); G=lambda V:V**1.75*((2**1.4+(a*V*V)**1.4)**(1/1.4)+B)/(a*V*V); print(min((G(0.5+i*1e-4),0.5+i*1e-4) for i in range(10000))[1]) prints 0.961 (the constant factor of the water gradient does not move the minimum).
