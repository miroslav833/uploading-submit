The figure (506 x 132 px) is a simulated map of the plasma electron density with two red marks above it, and one integer is read from it. The mark D (driver) stands at x = 476.5 px and the mark W (witness) at x = 44.5 px, 432.0 px apart. To the right of D the map is uniform. To the left of D it is striped with a wavelength of 5.24 px, so the marks are 82.5 wavelengths apart. Between the marks lie 83 dark stripes, with centres from x = 45.8 px to 475.7 px, and 82 bright stripes, from 48.4 px to 472.6 px. The first stripe behind D is dark and starts right at D. W stands a quarter of a wavelength to the left of a dark stripe and a quarter to the right of a bright one. So the witness rides the 83rd accelerating crest, N = 83, and its phase in the plateau is 165 pi.

Key by hand: behind a thin wide sheet of electrons the field is E = E_m cos(k_p xi), with E_m = sigma/eps0 = Q/(pi R^2 eps0) = 3.1956 GV/m whatever the density. The force on an electron, -eE, accelerates most at k_p xi = (2N - 1) pi, and the density minima lie a quarter of a wavelength in front of these crests, so N dark stripes lie between the driver and crest N. In the ramp each layer oscillates at its local plasma frequency and the witness arrives a fixed time later, so the phase is 165 pi u with u = sqrt(1 + 0.0227 z/L). Then the energy change is -e E_m (2L/0.0227) [u sin(165 pi u)/(165 pi) + cos(165 pi u)/(165 pi)^2] between u = 1 and u = 1.011286. The phase at the exit is 165 pi + 5.8504 rad, its sine is 0.41940 and its cosine -0.90780, the bracket is 0.0008186, and with e E_m L = 223.69 MeV the change is -0.07212 x 223.69 = -16.13 MeV, that is -16 MeV.

The most tempting wrong answer is 224 MeV. It is e E_m L, the gain of an electron that stays on the crest, as in a uniform plasma. Other wrong routes: sigma/(2 eps0) as the amplitude gives -8.1 MeV, 82 crests give -19 MeV and 84 give -13 MeV, a plasma frequency proportional to the density gives -14 MeV, a witness on a decelerating crest (166 pi) gives +15 MeV.

Check in a second (Python 3, standard library). It prints e E_m L, the key by Simpson's rule and the value for 82 crests, in MeV:
import math
W0 = 2.000e-9/(math.pi*150.0e-6**2*8.8541878128e-12)*70.00e-3/1e6
def dW(N, m=20000):
    f = lambda s: -math.cos((2*N - 1)*math.pi*math.sqrt(1 + 0.02270*s))
    return W0*(f(0) + f(1) + sum((4 if i % 2 else 2)*f(i/m) for i in range(1, m)))/(3*m)
print(round(W0, 2), round(dW(83), 2), round(dW(82), 2))   # 223.69 -16.13 -18.8
