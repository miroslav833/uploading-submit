By hand. Both faces are pressure antinodes and the sheets are the nodes, so L = N lambda/2: lambda = 2 x 45.00 mm/123 = 0.7317 mm, k = 8587 1/m. kappa0 = 1/(997.0 x 1497^2) = 4.476e-10 1/Pa, f1 = 1 - 2.49/4.476 = 0.4437, f2 = 2(1050 - 997.0)/(2 x 1050 + 997.0) = 0.03423, Phi = f1/3 + f2/2 = 0.1650. Gor'kov force F = 4 pi Phi k a^3 E_ac sin(2kz) against Stokes drag 6 pi eta a dz/dt gives tan(kz) growing as exp(4 Phi (ka)^2 E_ac t/(3 eta)). From kz = 0.1 pi to 0.4 pi: ln(3.0777/0.3249) = 2.2484. With a = 2.500 um, ka = 0.02147: E_ac = 3 x 0.890e-3 x 2.2484/(4 x 0.1650 x 0.02147^2 x 2.498) = 7.90 J/m^3, so 7.9.

The most tempting wrong answer is 2.0 J/m^3, from taking the sheet spacing as a whole wavelength (k half as large). Dropping the density term (Phi = f1/3) gives 8.8. 122 sheets give 8.0 and 124 give 7.8; a drive taken as exactly 2 MHz gives 8.3.

Check (Python, standard library):
import math
k0 = 1/(997.0*1497**2); f1 = 1 - 2.49e-10/k0; f2 = 2*(1050 - 997.0)/(2*1050 + 997.0); phi = f1/3 + f2/2
ka = 2*math.pi/(2*45.00e-3/123)*2.500e-6
lnr = math.log(math.tan(0.4*math.pi)/math.tan(0.1*math.pi))
print(round(3*0.890e-3*lnr/(4*phi*ka**2*2.498), 2))    # 7.9 J/m^3
