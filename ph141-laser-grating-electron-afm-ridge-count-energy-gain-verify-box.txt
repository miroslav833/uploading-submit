Key by hand: 67 ridges have 66 spacings, so the spacing is 64.16/66 = 0.97212 um and K = 6.4634 /um. The electron has gamma = 1 + 80/511 = 1.15656 and beta = 0.50240, so omega/v = 2 pi/(beta lambda) = 6.1007 /um. The energy change in one passage is -e times the integral of E_x along the path, taken at the time at which the electron is at each point. This picks out the Fourier component of the field with wavenumber omega/v along x. In vacuum this component decays with height with the constant sqrt((omega/v)^2 - (omega/c)^2) = omega/(v gamma) = 5.2749 /um, so 500 nm above the ridge tops it is smaller by exp(-2.6374) = 0.07154. In the plane of the ridge tops the m = 1 term with its Gaussian envelope gives E1 sqrt(pi) w exp(-(K - omega/v)^2 w^2/4) = 1047.5 V x exp(-1.1840) = 1047.5 V x 0.3061. All other terms have exponents of 335 or more. The largest gain is 1047.5 eV x 0.3061 x 0.07154 = 22.9 eV, that is 23 eV.

The most tempting wrong answer is 19 eV. It uses the decay constant of an unbounded grating wave, sqrt(K^2 - (omega/c)^2) = 5.690 /um, for the height factor: 1047.5 eV x 0.3061 x exp(-2.845) = 18.6 eV. Other wrong routes: no mismatch at all 75 eV, 67 spacings for 67 ridges 11 eV, no height factor 320 eV. Other counts: 66 ridges 40 eV, 68 ridges 11 eV, 65 ridges 58 eV.

Check in a second (Python 3, standard library). It prints the key and the value of the most tempting wrong route in eV:
import math
g = 1 + 80.00/511.0; b = math.sqrt(1 - 1/g**2)
K = 2*math.pi*66/64.16; k0 = 2*math.pi/2.050; kv = k0/b
A = 98.5*math.sqrt(math.pi)*6.00*math.exp(-((K - kv)*6.00/2)**2)
print(round(A*math.exp(-0.500*kv/g), 2), round(A*math.exp(-0.500*math.sqrt(K*K - k0*k0)), 2))   # 22.94 18.63
