Key: BGC0002390. It stands in the attached notes in the output of the first script (line KEY) and in the last line of the output of the second. By hand: the line still makes beta-macrocarpene and beta-bisabolene, so the terpene synthase locus works; it still hydroxylates supplied zealexin A1, so the P450 locus that acts on zealexin A1 works; it makes no carboxylic sesquiterpenoid, so the lost step is the oxidation of beta-macrocarpene to zealexin A1, the work of the three P450 genes zx5 to zx7 (Ding et al., Nat. Plants 6, 1375 (2020)). MIBiG 4.0 lists that locus as BGC0002390, product zealexin A1. The most tempting wrong answer is BGC0002389, the retired entry of the four beta-macrocarpene synthase genes zx1 to zx4, taken when the hydrocarbon build-up is read as the mark of that locus; a line without those genes has no beta-macrocarpene. BGC0002391 is the entry with the products zealexin B3 and C2. Check without the notes: open https://mibig.secondarymetabolites.org/repository/BGC0002390 and read organism, compound and genes; or unpack mibig_json_4.0.tar.gz and run in Python: import json; d=json.load(open('mibig_json_4.0/BGC0002390.json')); print(d['taxonomy']['name'], [c['name'] for c in d['compounds']]), which prints Zea mays ['zealexin A1'].
