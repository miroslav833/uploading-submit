Bright and dark ridges of the residual map between 30 and 120 km/s from the line centre (km/s): t = 1.0 h: +45.0, +69.5, +90.5, +108.5 and -105.5, -87.5, -64.5, -43.0; t = 2.0 h: +54.5, +76.5, +94.5, +111.5 and -117.0, -99.5, -81.0, -59.5, -35.5; t = 3.0 h: +34.5, +58.5, +80.5, +99.0, +118.5 and -113.0, -97.0, -74.0, -55.0, -31.0. The stripes slant to positive velocity as time grows (the bright ridge at +76.5 km/s at 2.0 h is at +99.0 km/s at 3.0 h). Taking the phase as m arcsin(v/V) plus a constant on the flanks, neighbouring bright and dark ridges on a flank are pi/|m| apart in arcsin(v/V), V = 150 km/s. A straight-line fit on each of the six flanks gives |m| = 18.7, 19.1, 20.3, 19.3, 18.7, 19.4, mean 19.25 +- 0.24, so l = |m| = 19. Disk integrations measured the same way give 18.43, 19.20, 19.97 for m = 18, 19, 20. Key by hand: R = V P/2 pi = 150 km/s x 0.9444 d/2 pi = 1.948e9 m; GM/R^3 = 2.920e20/(1.948e9)^3 = 3.950e-8 s^-2. Pattern speed for the observer 15.67/19 = 0.825 turns per day, slower than the rotation 1/P = 1.059, so the mode is retrograde on the star: nu_c = 19/0.9444 - 15.67 = 4.449 per day, omega_c^2 = (2 pi 4.449/86400)^2 = 1.047e-7 s^-2. With the Cowling approximation and delta p = 0 at the surface, xi_h/xi_r = GM/(omega_c^2 R^3) = 3.950e-8/1.047e-7 = 0.377, so 0.38. Most tempting wrong answer: 0.25, from m = 20 (ridge spacing read 5 per cent short; nu_c = 5.51 per day looks round). Others: m = 25 gives 0.064 (nu_c = 10.80 per day, also round-looking), m = 18 gives 0.65, m = 16 (all bands across the profile counted) gives 4.6, the observed frequency used directly gives 0.030. Check in Python: import math; R=150e3*0.9444*86400/(2*math.pi); nuc=19/0.9444-15.67; print(2.20*1.3271e20/((2*math.pi*nuc/86400)**2*R**3)) prints 0.3774.
