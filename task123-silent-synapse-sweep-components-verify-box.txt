The answer cannot be looked up. The papers below describe failure analysis with minimal stimulation and synapses that have NMDA receptors only. The number depends on the sweeps drawn in the figure.

Sweeps read from the figure. Failures (9): 7, 20, 22, 27, 29, 34, 35, 38, 40. Slow component alone (12): 1, 5, 6, 8, 10, 13, 14, 15, 17, 19, 31, 39. Fast and slow component (19): 2, 3, 4, 9, 11, 12, 16, 18, 21, 23, 24, 25, 26, 28, 30, 32, 33, 36, 37. Sweeps 7, 20 and 29 hold a current that begins 41, 25 and 16 ms after the stimulus and not at 2 to 3 ms, so it is spontaneous and the trial is a failure. Sweep 39 has a small slow response at the right latency and a spontaneous current 146 ms after the stimulus.

Key by hand. With n_A synapses with AMPA receptors, n_S without and release probability p, P(failure) = (1-p)^(n_A+n_S) and P(no fast component) = P(failure) + P(slow alone) = (1-p)^(n_A). So the fraction without AMPA receptors is 1 - ln(21/40)/ln(9/40) = 1 - 0.644357/1.491655 = 0.568.

The most tempting wrong answer is 0.387, the share of slow responses among the responses (12/31). It leaves out that a trial in which both kinds of synapse release looks like a dual response. Counting the spontaneous currents of sweeps 7, 20 and 29 as responses gives 0.608. Sweeps 9 and 23 have the smallest fast components (about 8 pA on a slow component of 11 pA); taking sweep 9 as slow gives 0.599 and both give 0.629. The fit in the notes finds a fast amplitude of at least 5.9 pA in every dual response and at most 2.7 pA in every slow response.

Check with Python (standard library):
import math
F = [7, 20, 22, 27, 29, 34, 35, 38, 40]
S = [1, 5, 6, 8, 10, 13, 14, 15, 17, 19, 31, 39]
print(round(1 - math.log((len(F) + len(S)) / 40) / math.log(len(F) / 40), 3), round(len(S) / (40 - len(F)), 3))
It prints 0.568 0.387.
