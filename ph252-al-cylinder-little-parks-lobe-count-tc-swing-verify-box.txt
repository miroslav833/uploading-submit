The answer is not taken from a paper. It follows from the givens and from one count on the record, so the content of the record is listed here as text.

What the record shows. The image is 866 x 300 px. The curve starts at x = 46 (zero field) and ends at x = 831 (20.1 mT). It is a train of sharp resistance maxima at equal spacing on a background that rises with the square of the field. There are 137 maxima: the first at x = 49, then one every 5.71 px (x = 49, 55, 60, 66, 72, 78, 83, 89, 95, 100, 106, ...); the 11th, 21st, 31st, ... 131st are at x = 106, 163, 220, 277, 334, 392, 449, 506, 563, 620, 677, 734, 791, and the 137th is at x = 825. The Fourier transform of the curve with the background removed has its strongest component at a period of 5.710 px. The sweep of 785 px holds 137.4 periods.

The key by hand. Period 20.1 mT/137.4 = 0.1463 mT. One flux quantum h/2e = 2.068e-15 Wb enters the mean cross-section per period, so r^2 = 2.068e-15/(pi x 1.463e-4) = 4.500e-12 m^2, r = 2.12 um. Little-Parks shift: 1 - Tc/Tc0 = (xi(0)/r)^2 (n - Phi/Phi0)^2, largest value (xi(0)/r)^2/4 at half-integer flux. Dirty limit: xi(0)^2 = 0.855^2 x 1.60e-6 x 40.0e-9 = 4.679e-14 m^2 (xi(0) = 216 nm). Swing = 1.31 K x 4.679e-14/(4 x 4.500e-12) = 3.405 mK, that is 3.4 mK. In terms of the count the swing is 467.9 mK/N: 136, 137, 138, 139 periods give 3.44, 3.42, 3.39, 3.37 mK; 135 gives 3.47 and 140 gives 3.34.

Wrong answers that come up easily. 1.7 mK: flux quantum h/e, or half the swing quoted as an amplitude. 14 mK: (xi(0)/r)^2 without the factor 1/4. 4.7 mK: xi(0)^2 = xi0 l without 0.855^2. 3.5 or 3.3 mK: 131 to 135 or 140 to 144 periods. 3.8 to 4.9 mK: the spacing of the maxima judged by eye as 6.4 to 8 px instead of 5.71 px (95 to 122 periods).

Check (Python, standard library):
import math
phi0 = 6.626e-34/(2*1.602e-19)
r2 = phi0/(math.pi*20.1e-3/137.4)
print(1e3*1.31*0.855**2*1.60e-6*40.0e-9/(4*r2))    # 3.405

A second check in the notes averages the pair velocity over a wall of finite thickness (0.05, 1.4 and 3 per cent of the radius) and minimises over the fluxoid number numerically; after the slow rise is removed the swing is 3.40 to 3.41 mK in every case.

Sources for the method: Little and Parks, Phys. Rev. Lett. 9, 9 (1962); Parks and Little, Phys. Rev. 133, A97 (1964); Groff and Parks, Phys. Rev. 176, 567 (1968); Tinkham, Introduction to Superconductivity, sections 4.2 and 4.5.
