The answer is not taken from a paper. It follows from the givens, one count on the record and the quantum resonance of the kicked rotor, so the content of the record is listed here as text.

What the record shows. The image is 866 x 230 px, a dark trace on a light screen without graticule or labels. A flat baseline at row 196 carries narrow pulses of equal height (about 155 px), with a small undershoot after each; the time axis is not labelled. There are 131 pulses, the first at x = 47.5 px and the last at x = 814.5 px, spaced 5.90 px (5.6 to 6.0 px). Every tenth pulse from the left is at x = 48, 107, 166, 225, 284, 343, 402, 460, 520, 579, 638, 697, 756, 815 px.

The key by hand. One flash imprints exp(-i kappa cos 2kx) with kappa = U0 tau/(2 hbar) = pi x 19.45 kHz x 1.600 us = 0.09777. Between flashes the order m (momentum 2 hbar k m) gains exp(-i 2 pi m^2 T/T_T) = 1 at T = T_T, so the N flashes act as one of strength N kappa = 131 x 0.09777 = 12.807. Zero order: J0^2(12.807) = 0.0359, that is 3.6 per cent. Half a unit in the last digit of the depth or the pulse length gives 3.575 to 3.608 per cent.

Numerical check. The notes propagate the orders -60 to 60 flash by flash with the exact Bessel couplings and the free phases: 0.03592 at T = T_T (norm kept to 1e-6); at 0.98 T_T the zero order would hold 79 per cent.

Wrong answers that come up easily. 2.3 per cent: kappa = U0 tau/hbar (the full depth instead of the cos 2kx amplitude). 53 per cent: the kicks treated as independent, J0^2(kappa)^N. 50 per cent: diffusive growth, J0^2(kappa sqrt N). Other values: a different number of pulses.

Check (Python with SciPy):
from scipy.special import jv
from math import pi
print(jv(0, 131*pi*19.45e3*1.600e-6)**2)    # 0.0359
