Data read from the image (464 x 464 px). Atomic columns found: 3785, which is (n + 1)^2 + n^2 for n = 43 cells. Outermost row along the bottom edge, x in px: 27, 37, 47, 56, 66, 76, 85, 94, 103, 112, 121, 130, 139, 148, 157, 166, 174, 183, 191, 200, 208, 216, 224, 231, 239, 247, 255, 263, 272, 280, 289, 298, 307, 318, 328, 338, 348, 359, 370, 380, 391, 402, 412, 422. That is 44 corner columns, so N = 43 cells per edge. The left edge also has 44. The spacing runs from 7 to 11 px because the specimen drifted during the scan.

Key by hand. V = (43 x 0.2866 nm)^3 = 1.8717e-24 m^3. For K1 > 0 the minima in zero field are the six <100> directions; the field along [001] keeps [001] and [00-1] on the axis and tilts the four in-plane minima towards [001] by about 3.0 deg, which changes their energy only at second order. The minimum at [00-1] has four equivalent saddles toward the in-plane minima, at the <110>-type directions such as (1, 0, -1)/sqrt 2, where the anisotropy energy is K1 V/4. To first order in the field the barrier is K1 V/4 - mu0 Ms H V (1 - 1/sqrt 2) = 2.2460e-20 - 0.2796e-20 = 1.9664e-20 J, so Delta E/kB = 1424.3 K. The dwell time is exp(Delta E/kB T)/(4 f0) = 5.0 ns, so Delta E/kB T = ln 200 = 5.298 and T = 268.8 K, which is 270 K to two figures. The exact saddle changes this by 0.6 per cent (270.5 K).

Most tempting wrong answer: 360 K, from one saddle instead of four (ln 50 = 3.912). One cell more or fewer gives 290 or 250 K.
