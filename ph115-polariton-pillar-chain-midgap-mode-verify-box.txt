Key by hand. With couplings J (short) and J' = 2J/3 (long) the zero-energy equations J' psi_0 + J psi_2 = 0, J' psi_2 + J psi_4 = 0, ... give psi_2m = (-2/3)^m psi_0 going clockwise, and the same going counter-clockwise. The ring is odd, so the clockwise tail fills the even-numbered pillars and the counter-clockwise tail the odd-numbered ones: every pillar has the intensity (2/3)^d relative to pillar 0, d being the even one of its two step counts. The sum over the ring is 1 + 2 [(4/9) + (4/9)^2 + ... + (4/9)^20] = 2.600. For A, d = 24: (2/3)^24 = 5.940e-5, and 5.940e-5/2.600 = 2.285e-5. The only equation that is not met is the one at pillar 0, by 2 J' (2/3)^20 psi_0, which puts the state at 1.5e-4 J, deep in the gap of half-width J/3. The exact eigenvector of the 41 x 41 matrix gives 2.280e-5. Answer 2.3e-5.

The most tempting wrong answer is 3.9e-4: decay along the shorter way round, (2/3)^17/2.6. Other values: the shorter way with 16 or 18 steps 5.9e-4 or 2.6e-4; A taken to lie on an empty sublattice 0; 22 or 26 steps on the long way 5.1e-5 or 1.0e-5; no normalisation 5.9e-5; one tail only in the sum 3.3e-5. When pillar 0 is not found and the exceptional pillar is placed at or next to A, the result is of order 0.1: 0.38 (A itself), 0.076 (four steps away), 0.12 or 0.28 (three steps or one step from a pillar with two short distances). The key is four orders of magnitude below these. The number belongs to the stated model: a shift of the on-site energy of pillar 0 by 0.03 J or a second-neighbour coupling of 0.03 J changes it by 11 to 15 per cent.

Check in a second (Python 3, standard library):
import math
N, r, kA = 41, 2/3, 17
t = [r if i % 2 == 0 else 1.0 for i in range(N)]      # t[i] couples pillars i and i+1; pillar 0 has r on both sides
H = lambda v: [t[i-1]*v[i-1] + t[i]*v[(i+1) % N] for i in range(N)]
v = [1.0] + [0.0]*(N - 1)
for _ in range(3000):                                 # power iteration on 2.8 - H^2 picks the state nearest E = 0
    v = [2.8*a - b for a, b in zip(v, H(H(v)))]
    s = math.sqrt(sum(a*a for a in v)); v = [a/s for a in v]
print(v[kA]**2, r**24/2.6, r**17/2.6)                 # 2.2805e-05, 2.2847e-05, 3.9037e-04
