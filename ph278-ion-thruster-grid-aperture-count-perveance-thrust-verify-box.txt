The answer is not taken from a paper; the engine is invented. It follows from the givens and from one reading of the figure, so that reading is listed here as text.

What the figure shows. The photograph is 1010 x 500 px, a strip through the middle of the grid. Bright apertures on a dark grid lie on a hexagonal lattice with rows nearly horizontal (turned 0.6 deg) and a pitch of 9.20 px. The perforated circle is 956.8 px across; its top and bottom lie outside the strip. The row through its centre is the longest, with 105 apertures from x = 27.8 to x = 984.6 px (y about 245 to 255); the rows directly above and below hold 104 each, the next ones 103. Along the central row the 1st, 11th, 21st, ..., 101st apertures are at x = 27.8, 119.8, 211.8, 303.8, 395.8, 487.8, 579.8, 671.8, 763.8, 855.8, 947.8 px, and the 105th at 984.6 px.

The key by hand. 105 apertures span 104 pitches: p = 22.50 cm/104 = 2.1635 mm. Apertures = pi x 0.1125^2 x 2/(sqrt3 p^2) = 9809. V_T = 1000 + 180 = 1180 V. l_e = sqrt(0.850^2 + 0.800^2) mm = 1.1673 mm. J = (4 eps0/9) sqrt(2e/M) V_T^1.5/l_e^2 = 141.93 A/m^2; per aperture J pi r_s^2 = 0.2854 mA; beam 2.799 A. Exhaust speed sqrt(2e x 1000/M) = 38 338 m/s. Thrust = 2.799 A x M x 38 338/e = 146 mN.

The most tempting wrong answer. Taking the 105 apertures of the central row as 105 pitches gives p = 2.1429 mm and 149 mN. Other wrong answers: 126 mN with a square-lattice density, 114 mN with 1000 V in the Child-Langmuir law, 159 mN with 1180 V in the exhaust speed.

Check (Python, standard library):
import math
e, eps0, M = 1.602176634e-19, 8.8541878128e-12, 131.29*1.66053906660e-27
p = 0.225/104; n = math.pi*0.1125**2*2/(math.sqrt(3)*p**2)
le = math.hypot(0.850e-3, 0.800e-3)
J = 4*eps0/9*math.sqrt(2*e/M)*1180**1.5/le**2
I = n*J*math.pi*(0.8e-3)**2
print(n, I, I*math.sqrt(2*M*1000/e)*1e3)   # 9809 2.799 146.0

The notes also hold a program that finds every aperture in the image file, fits the lattice and finds 105 apertures, 104 pitches, along the central row.

Sources for the method: Child, Phys. Rev. 32, 492 (1911); Langmuir, Phys. Rev. 2, 450 (1913); Brophy, Rev. Sci. Instrum. 73, 1071 (2002).
