Printed in image.png: peak 1 at 15 nm with 40 % of the scattered intensity; peak 2 at 70 nm with 60 %. Stem: 633 nm, detection at 173 deg, water n 1.330, same dense silica, dilute.

Key: q = (4 pi 1.330 / 633 nm) sin 86.5 deg = 0.02635 nm-1. Sphere form factor P = [3(sin x - x cos x)/x^3]^2 with x = qR: 0.842 for 70 nm, 0.992 for 15 nm. Mass ratio m70/m15 = (60/40)(15/70)^3(0.992/0.842) = 0.0174, so 1.71 %, answer 1.7. Exact Mie for silica (1.457) in water: 1.68 %.
Most tempting wrong answer: the Rayleigh d^6 law without the form factor, (60/40)(15/70)^3 = 0.01476, so 1.45 % (1.5). Taking the intensity share as mass share gives 60.

Check, standard-library Python:
import math
q=4*math.pi*1.330/633*math.sin(math.radians(86.5))
P=lambda d:(3*(math.sin(q*d/2)-q*d/2*math.cos(q*d/2))/(q*d/2)**3)**2
m=[40/(15**3*P(15)), 60/(70**3*P(70))]
print(round(100*m[1]/sum(m),2))      # 1.71
print(round(100*(60/70**3)/(40/15**3+60/70**3),2))   # 1.45 without P

Sources: Rayleigh, Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A 90, 219 (1914); Bohren and Huffman (1983); Berne and Pecora (1976).
