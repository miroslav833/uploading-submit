Key: 31 min. In the attached notes it is the line 'KEY: 30.987 min' of the first script; the second script, a time-marching solution in kcal-m-hr units, prints 30.99 min. The film coefficient is the double helical ribbon fit of Ishibashi, Yamanaka and Mitsuishi, J. Chem. Eng. Japan 12, 230 (1979), p. 232: Nu_w = 2.0 Re^1/3 Pr^1/3 Vis^-0.14 for 5 <= Re <= 50 (Eq. 15) and Nu_w = 1.0 Re^1/2 Pr^1/3 Vis^-0.14 for 50 <= Re <= 500 (Eq. 16), with Nu_w = h_w d/lambda on the impeller diameter and Vis = mu_w/mu_b (Nomenclature, p. 234). By hand: ln mu is linear in 1/T with slope 9362 K, so mu_w = 0.02091 Pa s at 200 C, and Re = n d^2 rho/mu runs from 6.27 at 100 C to 79.4 at 142 C, passing 50 at 133.66 C. At 100 C, Nu_w = 2.0 x 6.27^(1/3) x 56757^(1/3) x 0.00498^-0.14 = 297.8 and h_w = 92.4 W/m2K; it falls to 69.1 at 133.66 C, where Eq. (16) gives 66.3, and is 67.1 at 142 C. With M c_p/A = 2.568e5 J/m2K and t = (M c_p/A h) ln((T_w - T_a)/(T_w - T_b)) on the harmonic mean h of each range, 22.21 + 8.45 = 30.7 min; Simpson's rule gives 22.37 + 8.62 = 30.99 min. The most tempting wrong answer, 12.3 min, comes from putting the older helical ribbon correlation of Nagata et al., J. Chem. Eng. Japan 5, 83 (1972), Nu = 4.2 Re^1/3 Pr^1/3 Vis^-0.2 on D, in place of the 1979 fit: it puts h at 254 W/m2K at 100 C, nearly three times the 1979 value. An invented laminar form 1.5 Re^1/2 Pr^1/3 Vis^-0.2 on D gives 20 min the same way.
