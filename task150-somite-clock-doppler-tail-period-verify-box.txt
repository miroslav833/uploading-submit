The answer depends on the kymograph in the figure; it cannot be looked up. Soroldoni et al. (2014) show that somites form faster than the tail-bud clock ticks when the PSM shortens (Doppler effect).

Figure (um, min, embryo frame): somite boundaries at (300, 20), (370, 55), (440, 90), (510, 125), (580, 160), (650, 195), (720, 230), one every 35 min, anterior end moving 2.0 um/min. Stripes 140 um apart, moving anteriorly at 2.0 um/min. Bright for 110 um behind the anterior end, then 0.16 of that height (noise 0.10 of full scale) back to the tail bud: 760 um at 0 min, 952 um at 240 min (0.8 um/min). The faint stripes and their end are clearest at full size between 650 and 1000 um.

By hand: T = 140/(u + 2.0). Tail bud, u = 0.8: 50 min. Anterior end, u = 2.0: 35 min. Fixed point: 70 min.
Wave count: 240/35 - (500 - 212)/140 = 4.80 tail-bud cycles in 240 min.
35 min comes from taking the end of the bright zone, which moves with the anterior end, for the tail bud.

Python:
for u in (0.8, 2.0, 0.0): print(u, round(140 / (u + 2.0), 1))
prints 50.0, 35.0, 70.0. The notes include a program that fits the travelling wave to image.png and finds the posterior end of the faint stripes at 781 um + 0.73 um/min x t, giving 52 min, 50 to the nearest 5 min.

Sources: D. Soroldoni et al., Science 345, 222-225 (2014), Figs. 2-4 and the Doppler relation in the main text; L. G. Morelli et al., HFSP J. 3, 55-66 (2009), Sections 2-3.
