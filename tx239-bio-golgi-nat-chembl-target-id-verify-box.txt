Key: CHEMBL4630856. It stands in section 2 of the attached notes (line Key) and again at the end of section 3, where it is found by a search for the enzyme's name. By hand: loss of acetylation at retained-methionine N-termini followed by Lys, Ala, Val, Met or Leu points to NatE or NatF; the Golgi localisation through a C-terminal segment, the absence from ribosomes, the transmembrane substrates, the fragmented Golgi and the lack of a fungal orthologue are NatF, catalytic subunit NAA60 (Aksnes et al., Cell Rep. 10, 1362, 2015). Human NAA60 is UniProt Q9H7X0, and its ChEMBL_37 SINGLE PROTEIN target record is CHEMBL4630856. Most tempting wrong answer: CHEMBL4630854, the record of NAA50 (NatE), which shares the substrate preference but is ribosome-bound and has a yeast orthologue. Public check: the ChEMBL target report card https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/explore/target/CHEMBL4630856 shows N-alpha-acetyltransferase 60, Homo sapiens, SINGLE PROTEIN, UniProt Q9H7X0.
