What the figure carries. 71 bright spots lie on a circle of 118 px radius, one per sphere; one diameter is 8.8 px and neighbouring spots are 0.93 to 1.93 diameters apart.

By hand. The track is 2 pi x 42.93 um = 269.74 um, or 84.398 diameters. With 71 spheres the free length F is 13.398 diameters, in 71 gaps. Hard spheres give every allowed configuration the same weight, so the gaps are uniform on the simplex of total length F, and k chosen gaps all exceed one diameter with probability (1 - k/13.398)^70. One more sphere fits when the largest gap exceeds a diameter: P = 71 (1 - x)^70 - C(71,2) (1 - 2x)^70 + C(71,3) (1 - 3x)^70 - ... with x = 0.07464, which is 0.31127 - 0.03025 + 0.00113 - 0.00002 = 0.2821, so 0.28.

The most tempting wrong answer is 0.31, the first term alone. Independent gaps give 0.27. One sphere fewer or more gives 0.41 or 0.17.

import math
n, sigma, R = 71, 3.196, 42.93
x = sigma/(2*math.pi*R - n*sigma)
print(round(sum((-1)**(k+1)*math.comb(n, k)*(1 - k*x)**(n - 1) for k in range(1, int(1/x) + 1)), 4))   # 0.2821
