Key: EC 3.5.99.16. It stands in section 2 of the attached notes (line KEY) and as the last line of section 3. By hand: dopamine and secologanin give two C-1 epimers. N-Deacetylisoipecoside, 1 alpha(S), goes on to protoemetine, cephaeline and emetine; N-deacetylipecoside, 1 beta(R), goes to ipecoside (Nomura, Quesada and Kutchan 2008). The protein of the question forms the epimer opposite to the emetine precursor, the R epimer, and is deacetylipecoside synthase (De-Eknamkul, Suttipanta and Kutchan 2000). The Enzyme List gives EC 3.5.99.16, deacetylipecoside synthase, the history 'created 2000 as EC 4.3.3.4, transferred 2024 to EC 3.5.99.16', and the ENZYME release of 4 November 2026 marks 4.3.3.4 as 'Transferred entry: 3.5.99.16'. Most tempting wrong answer: EC 4.3.3.4, deleted in 2024. Taking the product for the emetine precursor gives deacetylisoipecoside synthase, EC 3.5.99.15 (formerly EC 4.3.3.3). Check in Python: fate={'deacetylisoipecoside':'emetine','deacetylipecoside':'ipecoside'}; ec={'deacetylisoipecoside':'3.5.99.15','deacetylipecoside':'3.5.99.16'}; print([ec[p] for p in fate if fate[p]!='emetine']) prints ['3.5.99.16'].
