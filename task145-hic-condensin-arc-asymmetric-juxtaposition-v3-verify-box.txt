The answer depends on the map in the figure; it cannot be looked up. Wang et al. (2015, 2017) show the line of flank contacts from condensin loaded at parS, and Brandao et al. (2019) show it tilts when the two sides move at different rates.

Figure: circular chromosome, 4,000 kb, bins of 10 kb, parS at 400 kb. The extra line is 0.09 of the gray range over a scatter of 0.10 per bin, about 50 kb wide. Points (lower, higher coordinate, kb): (400, 400), (164, 800), (0, 1078), then (3928, 1200), (3751, 1500), (3456, 2000), (3161, 2500), fading near (3043, 2700). Easiest to see at full size between (3900, 1250) and (3100, 2600), and in the copy below the diagonal.

By hand: slope 0.59. 1,500 kb is 1,100 kb from parS. 400 - 0.59 x 1,100 = -249 kb = 3,751 kb, so 3750.
Equal rates give 3300 kb; no line passes (3300, 1500) or (2800, 2000).

Python:
for r in (0.59, 0.57, 0.61, 1.0): print(r, (400 - r * (1500 - 400)) % 4000)
prints 3751, 3773, 3729, 3300. The notes include a program that reads every bin of image.png and finds the strongest line from parS at slope 0.590 (14 standard deviations above other lines); the equal-rate line shows nothing.

Sources: X. Wang et al., Genes Dev. 29, 1661-1675 (2015), Figs. 1-2; X. Wang et al., Science 355, 524-527 (2017), Figs. 1-3; N. T. Tran et al., Cell Rep. 20, 2057-2071 (2017), Fig. 1; H. B. Brandao et al., PNAS 116, 20489-20499 (2019), Figs. 1-3.
