The answer cannot be looked up. Barer (1952) and Davies and Wilkins (1952) give the principle: the optical path difference of an object in an interference microscope equals its refractive index difference times its thickness, and the refractive index difference of a protein solution is the refractive index increment times the concentration. Zhao, Brown and Schuck (2011) give the increment of proteins. The number depends on the fringes drawn in the figure.

Read from the figure (image 1300 x 1000 px, rows counted from the top):
scale bar 160 px for 5 µm, so 32 px per µm
droplet: columns 404 to 876, rows 264 to 736, diameter 472 px = 14.75 µm, centre at column 640 and row 500
background: 29.4 fringes in 1000 px, spacing 34 px, bright fringes at rows ... 444, 478, 512, 546 ...
bright fringes on the vertical diameter of the droplet: rows 280, 301, 325, 351, 379, 408, 439, 471, 504, 539, 576, 616, 660, and a bright cap at row 724 next to the lower rim
the background fringe of row 512 enters at the left rim and crosses the vertical diameter at row 439

Key by hand. The fringe of row 512 has risen 73 px = 2.15 spacings where it crosses the diameter, 61 px above the centre. For a sphere the displacement there is 0.966 of the value at the centre, so the centre has 2.2 spacings. The count on the diameter gives the same: 6.45 periods between rows 311 and 500, 4.65 between rows 500 and 689, difference 1.80 = 0.8 x 2.25. Then 2.25 x 0.546 µm / 14.75 µm = 0.0833 and 0.0833 / 0.185 mL/g = 0.450 g/mL, which is 450 mg/mL.

The most tempting wrong answer is 50. It takes the quarter of a spacing seen at the centre as the whole displacement: 0.25 x 0.546 / 14.75 / 0.185 = 0.050 g/mL. One whole order gives 250 and three give 650. A fringe spacing read as about 24 px (40 fringes in the image height, where there are 29.4) leads to about four orders and to 750 or 850.

Check with Python (standard library):
from math import sqrt
R, s, D = 236, 34, 472 / 32
rise = (512 - 439) / s
phi0 = rise / sqrt(1 - ((500 - 439) / R) ** 2)
print(round(rise, 2), round(phi0, 2))
for p in (0.25, 1.25, 2.25, 3.25):
    print(p, round(0.8 * R / s + 0.4 * p, 2), round(0.8 * R / s - 0.4 * p, 2), round(p * 0.546 / D / 0.185 * 1000))
It prints 2.15 2.22, then for each trial displacement the periods above and below the centre and the concentration: 5.65 5.45 50, 6.05 5.05 250, 6.45 4.65 450, 6.85 4.25 650.
