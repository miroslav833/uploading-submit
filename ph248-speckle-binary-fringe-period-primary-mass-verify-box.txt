The answer is not taken from a paper; the star is not a named one. It follows from the givens and from one reading of the figure, so that reading is listed here as text.

What the figure shows. The map is the logarithm of the averaged power spectrum. In the image file (527 x 517 px) the plot frame runs from x = 70 to 507 and y = 20 to 457; the tick marks u = -60, -40, ..., 60 are at x = 121, 177, 233, 289, 345, 401, 457 and v = 60, 40, ..., -60 at y = 71, 127, ..., 407, so the scale is 2.80 px per cycle/arcsec and the origin is at pixel (289, 239). Straight cosine fringes cross the disc from upper left to lower right. Along the row v = 0 (y = 239) the maxima are at u = 3.813 k cycles/arcsec, x = 289 + 10.68 k px; the 5th, 10th, 15th and 18th on the right are at u = 19.07, 38.13, 57.20, 68.64 (x = 342.4, 395.8, 449.2, 481.2), the same on the left. Along the column u = 0 they are 5.872 apart (5th and 10th at v = 29.36 and 58.72). The disc edge is the cutoff 8.1 m/562 nm = 69.9 cycles/arcsec.

The key by hand. rho_u = 1/3.813 = 0.2623", rho_v = 1/5.872 = 0.1703", rho = 0.3127" (period across the fringes 3.198 cycles/arcsec, 43.7 periods across the disc). Circular orbit and the brighter star at its largest receding speed: the stars lie on the line of nodes and the projected separation is the full a. a = 0.3127/0.01948 = 16.05 AU. M1 + M2 = 16.05^3/34.90^2 = 3.396 solar masses. M1 = 3.396 x 6.21/11.07 = 1.905, i.e. 1.9. Check: a sin i = (K1+K2)P/2pi = 12.97 AU, sin i = 0.808, M1 sin^3 i = 1.005. Two figures hold for rho from 0.3097" to 0.3151".

The most tempting wrong answer. Taking the fringe spacing along the u axis (3.813) as the period gives rho = 0.2623", a = 13.46 AU, M1 + M2 = 2.004 and M1 = 1.12, i.e. 1.1. Other wrong answers: 2.2 from reading about 45 periods across the disc; 1.0 for an edge-on orbit (M1 sin^3 i); 1.5 with the mass ratio inverted; 3.4, the total mass.

Check (Python, standard library):
import math
rho = math.hypot(1/3.813, 1/5.872)
a = rho/0.01948
print(rho, a, a**3/34.90**2*6.21/11.07)   # 0.3127 16.05 1.905

The notes also hold a program that reads the tick marks and the fringes from the image file alone and returns rho = 0.3127" at 33.0 deg from the u axis.

Sources for the method: Labeyrie, A&A 6, 85 (1970); Korff, JOSA 63, 971 (1973); Horch et al., AJ 150, 151 (2015); Pourbaix, A&AS 131, 377 (1998).
