Key: 726050. It stands in section 2 of the attached notes (line KEY, the child of ORPHA:3319 whose gene is THPO) and again in section 3, where the gene is chosen from the thrombopoietin feedback loop. By hand, an amegakaryocytic marrow from birth that later fails in all lines is congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia. Thrombopoietin is made by the liver and cleared by MPL, so the receptor form has very high levels; an undetectable level at a platelet count of 9 x 10^9/L means the hormone is not made, and full recovery on romiplostim shows the receptor works. The homozygous variant lies in a known causal gene, which with these two findings must be THPO. This is the THPO form, which Orphanet (Orphadata June 2026) lists as ORPHA:726050 under ORPHA:3319, with no entry below it. The most tempting wrong answer is 3319, the disorder entry, which is right but not the narrowest; taking the commonest cause gives 726047, the MPL form. Check in Python with a one-compartment model of the hormone (production P by the liver, clearance by MPL in proportion to receptor mass R): tpo=lambda P,R: P/(0.05+R); print({'MPL form':round(tpo(1,0.02),1),'THPO form':tpo(0,1),'normal':round(tpo(1,1),2)}) gives about 14 for the receptor form, 0 for the ligand form and 0.95 for normal, so an undetectable level points to the THPO child of 3319, ORPHA:726050.
