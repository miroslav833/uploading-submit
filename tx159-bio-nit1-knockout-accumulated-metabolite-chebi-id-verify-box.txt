Key: CHEBI:138934. It stands in section 2 of the attached notes (line KEY) and again in section 3, where the entry is chosen from its SMILES by atom balance and charge. By hand: Peracchi et al., PNAS 114, E3233 (2017) show that Nit1 knockout mice excrete deaminated glutathione in large amounts in urine, that Nit1 knockout cells accumulate it, and that Nit1 hydrolyses it to 2-oxoglutarate and L-cysteinylglycine. Deaminated glutathione is N-(4-oxoglutaryl)-L-cysteinylglycine, C10H14N2O7S (glutathione C10H17N3O6S minus N and 3 H plus O). ChEBI lists the uncharged molecule as CHEBI:138934 and the dianion, C10H12N2O7S, as CHEBI:138256. The uncharged form asked for is CHEBI:138934. Most tempting wrong answer: CHEBI:138256, the dianion that Rhea and UniProt use for the reaction at pH 7.3. Taking Nit1 for an omega-amidase like Nit2 gives 2-oxoglutaramic acid, CHEBI:30882. Guessing the neutral entry as the number just below the dianion gives CHEBI:138255, which is an unrelated epoxy fatty acid.
