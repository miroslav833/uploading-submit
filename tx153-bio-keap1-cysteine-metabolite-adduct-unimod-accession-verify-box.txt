Key: 2117. It stands in section 1 of the attached notes (line KEY) and again in section 2. By hand: ACOD1 makes itaconate (C5H6O4). A cysteine thiol adds across its C=C by Michael addition, nothing is lost, and the residue gains C5H6O4, 130.0266 Da (S-2,3-dicarboxypropyl), as Mills et al., Nature 556, 113 (2018) showed for KEAP1. In unimod.xml two cysteine records carry H(6) C(5) O(4): 1253, 2-monomethylsuccinyl (adduct of methyl hydrogen fumarate, a different isomer) and 2117, Itaconatyl, whose source is Cifani et al., J. Proteome Res. 20, 1835 (2021). The answer is 2117. Most tempting wrong answer: 2118, the record titled Itaconyl, which is the acyl form (C5H4O3, 112.0160 Da) on lysine, not the cysteine adduct. Runs that get the formula right but do not know record 2117 give 1253. Check in Python: rows=[(1253,'C','H(6) C(5) O(4)','2-monomethylsuccinyl'),(2117,'C','H(6) C(5) O(4)','Itaconatyl'),(2118,'K','H(4) C(5) O(3)','Itaconyl')]; print([r[0] for r in rows if r[1]=='C' and r[2]=='H(6) C(5) O(4)' and 'Itacon' in r[3]]) prints [2117]. Citations: Mills et al., Nature 556, 113 (2018); Michelucci et al., PNAS 110, 7820 (2013); Cifani et al., J. Proteome Res. 20, 1835 (2021); Liu et al., JACS 145, 12673 (2023); Creasy and Cottrell, Proteomics 4, 1534 (2004).
