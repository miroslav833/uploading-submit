Measured from the pixels of the figure (mean grey level in a window 0.22 mm wide at the arrow, 1.0 mm temporal): outer plexiform layer ends at 376 um, external limiting membrane at 480 um. A two-level fit of the dark band between them splits it at 447 um: mean grey 36.5 above and 25.6 below (8-bit), a step of 11 grey levels. At 1.0 mm nasal the same fit finds no step inside the band (only the blurred edge of the outer plexiform layer at 390 um). The lighter inner part on the temporal side is the Henle fibre layer, seen obliquely by the beam; the darker part next to the external limiting membrane is the layer of somata. Key: 480 - 445 = 35 um (model 35.1 um; the read-back gives 33 at full size and 34 at half size), to the nearest 5 um 35. The most tempting wrong answer takes the whole dark band, 480 - 375 = 105 um, the usual on-axis OCT 'outer nuclear layer', which includes about 70 um of Henle fibres. Where to look: the dark band 0.3 to 1.6 mm temporal, best seen by comparing it with the same band on the nasal side. Check in Python: opl, split, elm = 375, 445, 480; print(elm - split, elm - opl).
