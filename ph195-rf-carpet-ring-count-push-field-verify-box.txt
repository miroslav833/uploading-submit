By hand. Pitch d = 13.71 mm/66 = 0.20773 mm, k = pi/d = 15124 1/m, kh = 4.537. Field magnitude of the fundamental (4 V0/pi) k exp(-kz) = 2.407e6 V/m x exp(-4.537) = 2.577e4 V/m at h (the third harmonic is down by exp(-2kh) = 1.2e-4). Mobility K = 18.2 x (1013.25/110)(297/273.15) = 182.28 cm2/(V s), gamma = q/(mK) = 3.983e7 1/s, Omega = 3.770e7 1/s. Effective potential with damping U* = q^2 E^2/[4m(Omega^2 + gamma^2)], so U*(h)/q = 0.04006 V. Balance 2k U*(h) = q E_push gives E_push = 1212 V/m = 12.1 V/cm, which is 12 V/cm to two figures. The givens moved by half a unit of their last digit keep it between 11.9 and 12.3 V/cm.

The most tempting wrong answer is 1.5e2 V/cm: since 13.71 = 30 x 0.457, it takes a pitch of exactly 0.457 mm (31 rings) instead of counting the 67 rings. Each ring more or less moves the answer by about 10 per cent (66 rings 13 V/cm, 68 rings 11 V/cm). Leaving out the damping gives 26 V/cm; the reduced mobility without the scaling to 110 hPa and 297 K gives 0.23 V/cm; a field period of one pitch instead of two gives 0.011 V/cm; a pitch rounded to 0.200 mm gives 9.6 V/cm.

A check in Python, standard library only.

import math
q, m = 1.602176634e-19, 132.905*1.66053906660e-27
N, span, V0, f, h = 67, 13.71e-3, 125.0, 6.00e6, 0.300e-3
k = math.pi/(span/(N - 1))
K = 18.2e-4*(1013.25/110)*(297/273.15)
g, W = q/(m*K), 2*math.pi*f
E = 4*V0/math.pi*k*math.exp(-k*h)
print(2*k*q*E**2/(4*m*(W*W + g*g))/100)   # 12.1 V/cm
