The plotted record is the only source of the axial frequency. It is a simulated image-current transient, 1100 x 340 px, made for this question; cursor A stands on a crest at column 64 and cursor B on a crest at column 1070 (columns from 0), and the question gives t_B - t_A = 645.05 us. Counting crests from A, every twentieth falls at about 158, 252, 346, 440, 534, 628, 722, 816, 910 and 1004 px, and B is the 214th: 214 periods of 4.70 px, f = 214/645.05 us = 331.76 kHz.

Key by hand. The ideal field is Phi = (k/2)(z^2 - r^2/2) + (k/2) Rm^2 ln(r/Rm), and the electrode through (R, 0) is z^2 = (r^2 - R^2)/2 + Rm^2 ln(R/r). The outer electrode passes through (12.400 mm, 0) and (7.270 mm, 11.000 mm), so Rm^2 = [121.000 + 50.4535]/0.533940 mm^2 = 321.110 mm^2 (Rm = 17.920 mm). The voltage gives 3870 V = (k/2)[Rm^2 ln(12.400/4.850) - (12.400^2 - 4.850^2)/2] mm^2 = (k/2)(236.313 mm^2), k = 3.2753e7 V/m^2. The axial motion is m z'' = -q k z at any radius and energy, and the current between the two halves of the outer electrode oscillates at omega_z = sqrt(q k/m). So m/q = k/omega_z^2 = 727.3 Da/e, which rounds to 727.

The most tempting wrong answer is 769, from taking Rm = sqrt(2) x 12.400 mm = 17.536 mm instead of using the second point of the outer electrode.

Check program (Python 3, standard library):
import math
R1, R2, L, re, U, dt = 4.850e-3, 12.400e-3, 11.000e-3, 7.270e-3, 3870.0, 645.05e-6
Rm2 = (L**2 - (re**2 - R2**2)/2)/math.log(R2/re)
k = 2*U/(Rm2*math.log(R2/R1) - (R2**2 - R1**2)/2)
w = 2*math.pi*214/dt
print(1.602176634e-19*k/w**2/1.66053906660e-27)   # 727.3
