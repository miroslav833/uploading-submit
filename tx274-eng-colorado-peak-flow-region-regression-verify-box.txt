Key: 1560 ft3/s. It stands in section 2 of the attached notes (line KEY) and again in section 3, which works the same equation in logarithms. By hand: the report fits its peak-streamflow equations separately for four regions defined by mean basin elevation above NAVD88, Alpine above 10,490 ft, Sub-Alpine 9,493 to 10,490 ft, Mid-Elevation 8,015 to 9,492 ft, Plateau below 8,014 ft. This basin, at 8,900 ft, is Mid-Elevation. Its 100-year equation is Q100 = 10^-7.33 AREA^0.837 10^(-0.0782 LONG + 0.0182 PPTANN). With AREA = 85.0 mi2, LONG = -107.40 degrees and PPTANN = 28.0 in: log10 Q = -7.33 + 1.6149 + 8.3987 + 0.5096 = 3.1932, so Q = 1560 ft3/s. All three predictors lie inside the ranges the report states. Most tempting wrong answer: 1100 ft3/s, from the Sub-Alpine equation 10^-5.56 AREA^0.841 10^(-0.0595 LONG + 0.142 P100Y24H + 0.0205 SWE), taken by placing the basin in the next band up. The Alpine equation gives 1270 ft3/s. Check in Python: import math; print(round(10**(-7.33+0.837*math.log10(85.0)-0.0782*(-107.40)+0.0182*28.0))) prints 1560.
