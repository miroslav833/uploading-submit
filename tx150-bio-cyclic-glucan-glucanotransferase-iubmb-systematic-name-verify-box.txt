Key: (1->2)-beta-D-glucan:(1->2)-beta-D-glucan 2-beta-D-[(1->2)-beta-D-glucano]-transferase (cyclizing and configuration-retaining)

You can read it in the IUBMB Enzyme List (ExplorEnz, enzyme-database.org), EC 2.4.1.397, cyclic beta-1,2-glucan glucanotransferase, created 2024, field "Systematic name". On the web page the arrows and Greek letters are printed as symbols; the question asks for them as -> and words. The attached notes copy this row from the ExplorEnz database dump (first script, line "KEY").

The name follows its family in the same list. The branching enzyme EC 2.4.1.18 is (1->4)-alpha-D-glucan:(1->4)-alpha-D-glucan 6-alpha-D-[(1->4)-alpha-D-glucano]-transferase, and the cyclizing enzyme of alpha-1,4-glucans, EC 2.4.1.19, is (1->4)-alpha-D-glucan:(1->4)-alpha-D-glucan 4-alpha-D-[(1->4)-alpha-D-glucano]-transferase (cyclizing). Swapping in the beta-(1->2) glucan and a new bond at O-2 in beta form gives the body of the key. The qualifier is set by the entry: the older cyclizing enzyme carries "(cyclizing)" alone, and the 2024 entry states ring closure and retention together as "(cyclizing and configuration-retaining)". The live entry page and the database dump print the same name (both copies are in the notes).

The most tempting wrong answer is the name with "(cyclizing)" alone, often also with the glucan written only once, (1->2)-beta-D-glucan 2-beta-D-[(1->2)-beta-D-glucano]-transferase (cyclizing). It copies the qualifier of the older cyclizing enzyme and leaves out the retention qualifier that this entry carries.

These Python 3 lines build the key from the EC 2.4.1.19 name.
cgt = '(1->4)-alpha-D-glucan:(1->4)-alpha-D-glucan 4-alpha-D-[(1->4)-alpha-D-glucano]-transferase (cyclizing)'
s = cgt.replace('(1->4)-alpha', '(1->2)-beta').replace(' 4-alpha', ' 2-beta').replace('(cyclizing)', '(cyclizing and configuration-retaining)')
print(s)

The sources are the IUBMB Enzyme List, EC 2.4.1.397; Tanaka et al., Appl. Microbiol. Biotechnol. 108, 187 (2024); Guidolin et al., J. Bacteriol. 191, 1230 (2009); McDonald and Tipton, FEBS J. 290, 2214 (2023).
