That is N = 47 + 45 + 41 = 133 stages.

By hand. In the pores molecules meet only walls, so the chance of crossing a membrane does not depend on speed or wall temperature, and the net molecule flow is proportional to p_c/sqrt(T_c) - p_w/sqrt(T_w). Calibrated with the conductance C at 295.0 K, the throughput measured at 295.0 K is Q = C (p_c - p_w/r), r = sqrt(325.0/295.0) = 1.04962. The ducts have uniform pressure, so p_(i+1) = r (p_i - Q/C) with Q/C = 2.34e-6/6.50e-7 = 3.600 Pa. Fixed point p* = r (Q/C)/(r - 1) = 76.16 Pa, and p_0 = p* + (p_N - p*) r^(-N). With r^133 = 626.7 and p_N = 101300 Pa: p_0 = 76.16 + 161.51 = 237.7 Pa = 0.24 kPa.

The most tempting wrong answer is 0.16 kPa: the zero-flow value 101300/626.7 = 161.6 Pa, which leaves the leak out. Subtracting Q/C after the compression instead of before it (p_w = r p_c - Q/C) gives 234.1 Pa, 0.23 kPa. 132 stages give 245.7 Pa and 134 give 230.0 Pa; 100 stages, a count taken from the row lengths with too wide a spacing, give 0.87 kPa.

Check (Python, standard library):
import math
r = math.sqrt(325.0/295.0); d = 2.34e-6/6.50e-7; p = 101300.0
for i in range(133): p = p/r + d
print(round(p, 1))    # 237.7 Pa, 0.24 kPa
