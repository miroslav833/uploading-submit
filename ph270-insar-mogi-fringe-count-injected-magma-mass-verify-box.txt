The answer is not taken from a paper; the volcano is not a named one. It follows from the givens and from one reading of the figure, so that reading is listed here as text.

What the figure shows. The image file is 1102 x 956 px. The map frame runs from x = 72 to 952 and y = 18 to 898, 55.0 px per km; V, the vent at (0, 0) km, is at pixel (512, 458), and G, the GNSS station at (6.43, -5.00) km, at pixel (866, 733). Colours are the motion toward the satellite relative to G, wrapped every 27.735 mm (half of 55.47 mm); G is red, the start of the colour bar. On the straight line from G to V the 5th, 10th, 15th, 20th and 25th red bands after G are crossed at (3.34, -2.59), (2.31, -1.80), (1.68, -1.31), (1.15, -0.90) and (0.57, -0.44) km. V lies after the 28th red band, in cyan, at 13.8 mm on the bar: 28.5 cycles. The centre of the bull's-eye of rings is at (-0.97, -0.21) km, about 31.2 cycles from G.

The key by hand. G did not move along the line of sight, so V moved 28.5 x 27.735 = 790.4 mm toward the satellite. At V only vertical motion projects, so the uplift is 790.4/0.781 = 1012.0 mm. Point source in a half-space: uz = (1 - nu) dVc/(pi d^2), so dVc = pi x 4200^2 x 1.0120/0.75 = 7.48e7 m^3. The magma is compressible: dVm = dVc (1 + 4 mu/3K) = 7.48e7 x (1 + 96.0/26.1) = 7.48e7 x 4.678 = 3.50e8 m^3, i.e. 3.5e8 m^3. Two figures hold for 28.11 to 28.92 cycles between G and V.

The most tempting wrong answer. Leaving out the compression of the magma gives the cavity volume change 7.5e7 m^3. Other wrong answers: 3.8e8 m^3 from counting the rings to the centre of the bull's-eye (31.2 cycles); 2.7e8 m^3 from taking the line-of-sight motion as the uplift; 3.4e8 or 3.6e8 m^3 from one cycle fewer or more.

Check (Python, standard library):
import math
uz = 28.5*27.735/0.781/1000
dvc = math.pi*4200**2*uz/0.75
print(dvc, dvc*(1 + 4*24.0/(3*8.70)))   # 7.48e7 3.50e8

The notes also hold a program that reads the axes, the colour bar and the colours from the image file alone and finds 28.47 cycles between G and V on three different paths.
