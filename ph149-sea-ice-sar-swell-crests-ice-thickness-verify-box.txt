The image is the only source of the wavelength. It is a simulated radar amplitude image, 500 x 190 px, made for this question. With pixel columns and rows numbered from 0, A is at (35.5, 94.5) px and B at (459.5, 94.5) px. Along that row the crests (bright maxima) lie at x = 35.5 + 8.0 j px for j = 0 to 53, so A and B are 53 wavelengths apart: every fifth crest from A is at 35.5, 75.5, 115.5, 155.5, 195.5, 235.5, 275.5, 315.5, 355.5, 395.5 and 435.5 px, and B is three crests further on.

Key by hand. lambda = 7089 m/53 = 133.755 m, k = 0.046975 1/m, omega = 2 pi/9.000 s = 0.698132 1/s. Deep-water flexural-gravity relation with the inertia of the ice: rho_w omega^2 = k (D k^4 + rho_w g - rho_i h omega^2), D = E h^3/(12 (1 - nu^2)). As a cubic in h: 117.655 h^3 - 20.9949 h - 27.2229 = 0 (SI units), that is h^3 - 0.178446 h - 0.231380 = 0. One real root (Cardano): h = cbrt(0.230467) + cbrt(0.000913) = 0.61311 + 0.09702 = 0.7101 m, which rounds to 0.71 m.

The most tempting wrong answer is 0.61 m, from leaving out the inertia of the ice (117.655 h^3 = 27.2229). Other crest counts: 52 wavelengths give 0.79 m, 54 give 0.63 m, 51 give 0.86 m, 50 give 0.94 m, 49 give 1.01 m, 56 give 0.39 m, and 57 or more give no positive thickness. Without 1 - nu^2 in D the answer would be 0.74 m.

Check program (Python 3, standard library):
import math
g, rw, ri, E, nu, T, AB, N = 9.810, 1025.0, 917.0, 5.50e9, 0.330, 9.000, 7089.0, 53
w2, k = (2*math.pi/T)**2, 2*math.pi*N/AB
f = lambda h: k*(E*h**3/(12*(1 - nu*nu))*k**4 + rw*g - ri*h*w2) - rw*w2
lo, hi = 0.01, 5.0
for _ in range(100):
    m = (lo + hi)/2
    lo, hi = (lo, m) if f(lo)*f(m) <= 0 else (m, hi)
print(lo)   # 0.7101 (m)
