That is 147 pulses, 5 to 13 px apart, so N = 147 clicks in 10.000 us.

Key by hand. Mean interval between clicks 10.000 us/147 = 68.027 ns. One interval is the dead time of 50.0 ns plus an exponential waiting time, so the mean waiting time is 68.027 - 50.0 = 18.027 ns (rate while armed 55.47 per us). Prescaler period, 288 intervals: mean 288 x 68.027 ns = 19.592 us. Spread from the waiting times: the dead time adds no variance, so sigma_s = sqrt(288) x 18.027 ns = 0.3059 us. Power drift: only the waiting part of the period, 288 x 18.027 ns = 5.192 us, scales with the power, so sigma_b = 5.192 x 0.030 = 0.1558 us. Total sigma = sqrt(0.3059^2 + 0.1558^2) = 0.3433 us. The limit is 20.00 - 19.592 = 0.408 us above the mean, 1.189 standard deviations, and the part of a Gaussian above that is 0.117, that is 0.12.

The most tempting wrong answer is 0.0059. It belongs to 150 clicks, which an estimate from the width of the frame and a typical spacing (good to a few per cent only) cannot tell from 147, and at which every quantity comes out round: mean interval 66.667 ns, waiting time 16.667 ns, mean period 19.200 us, sigma = sqrt(0.28284^2 + 0.14400^2) = 0.3174 us, limit 2.521 sigma above the mean, fraction 0.0059. For 144 clicks the mean period is 2880/144 = 20.000 us, on the limit, and the fraction is one half. One pulse more or less than 147: 146 clicks 0.22, 148 clicks 0.053. Other counts: 143 clicks 0.64, 145 clicks 0.35. At 147 clicks, the interval variance taken as the square of the mean interval (Poisson clicks at the observed rate) 0.36, the power drift applied to the whole period 0.27, both together 0.38, the power drift left out 0.091, the two spreads added linearly 0.19.

Check in a second (Python 3, standard library):
import math
T0, tau, n0, eps, t0 = 10.000, 0.0500, 288, 0.030, 20.00      # microseconds
for N in (147, 150, 144, 146, 148):
    w = T0/N - tau                                              # mean waiting time
    s = math.hypot(math.sqrt(n0)*w, n0*w*eps)
    print(0.5*math.erfc((t0 - n0*T0/N)/(s*math.sqrt(2))))      # 0.1172, 0.0059, 0.5000, 0.2183, 0.0531
