Key depends on whether the big rounded lobe on the right counts as invasion. It doesn't: it is lined by endometrial stroma all round, so it's carcinoma in adenomyosis (Singh et al. 2019, ISGyP, section "Myometrial Invasion From a Focus of Adenomyosis").

Read from the pixels of the figure (2880 x 2835 px, bar 450 px = 5 mm, 90 px per mm):
junction of the endometrium beside the tumour: row 900 (rows 883 to 918 along the wavy edge)
serosa: row 2705 under the tongue, 2702 under the lobe, so 20.0 mm of myometrium
left tongue, lowest gland: row 1167, 2.96 mm below the junction; pale band round it ends at 3.29 mm
right lobe, outer edge: row 1530, 6.98 mm below the junction
dark layer at the edge of the lobe: 6 px (0.06 mm), grey 107 of 255
other greys: endometrial stroma 102, stroma of the adenomyosis islands 99, tumour stroma 177, muscle 204, pale band 226

Where to look: lower edge of the right lobe at full size, about x 1300 to 2200, y 1400 to 1540 px. The dark layer is as dark as the endometrium, and no other part of the tumour has it.

Key: 100 x 2.96/20.0 = 14.8, so 15 %. Holds for a depth from 2.5 to 3.5 mm.
Likely wrong answer: lobe counted, 100 x 6.98/20.0 = 34.9, so 35 %.

Check, standard-library Python
r5 = lambda p: int(5*((p + 2.5)//5))
