By hand, with the paper's definitions (Nomenclature p. 1192: Gr = g beta dT S^3/nu^2, beta = 1/T_inf, Ra = Pr Gr S/H; properties at the surface temperature, p. 1193): Ra = 9.81 x 60 x 0.7154 S^4/(293.15 x (2.097e-5)^2 x 0.160) = 2.0416e10 S^4 (S in m). Eq. (22), p. 1198: Nu_f = (Ra/24)[1 - exp(-(32.7/Ra)^(3/4) f3)], f3 = 1 - (S/L)/Ra^(1/4) = 0.93386 for every S here. Eq. (7), p. 1194: Nu_b = (Ra/70)[1 - exp(-(57/Ra)^(2/3))][1 - exp(-10 (57/(4 Ra))^(1/4))]^(1/3). Eq. (12), p. 1196: Nu_c = [Nu_f + (S/2L) Nu_b]/(1 + S/2L). Heat per metre of width: q' = Nu_c (lambda/S)(2L + S) H dT/(S + t). At S = 12.6 mm: Ra = 514.57, Nu_f = 2.3902, Nu_b = 1.5056, Nu_c = 2.2698, alpha_c = 5.320 W/m2K, 4.729 W per channel, pitch 14.6 mm, q' = 323.90 W/m. At 12.5 mm: 325.34 W/m. Interpolation gives 12.593 mm. q' has its largest value 384.3 W/m at 7.66 mm and falls steadily beyond it, so this is the widest gap; the other root is 5.34 mm. Eq. (24), S/L < 0.2 Ra^(1/4), holds (0.315 against 0.952). Most tempting wrong answers: 14.9 mm, from the U-channel correlation of Van de Pol and Tierney (1973) with beta = 1/353.15 K, which is another correlation than the one named; and 16.5 mm, from the Elenbaas parallel-plate relation Nu = (Ra/24)[1 - exp(-35/Ra)]^(3/4) with beta = 1/323.15 K on the same area; with the paper's formulas but that beta the result is 11.9 mm.
