Key: 75 mA (75.5 mA before rounding to the nearest 5 mA). It stands in section 2 of the attached notes (line KEY) and in section 3 (line SAME KEY BY THIS ROUTE). Source: Iguchi, Tomida, Nakajima, Morita, ISIJ Int. 33, 728 (1993), Eq. (9), p. 733: Sh = 0.664 Re^(0.5 + 0.10 Tu) Sc^(1/3), Tu = u'_rms/u, Re = u L/nu on the mean velocity, valid for 1e2 < Re < 1e4 and 0.4 < Tu < 5. By hand: Re = 0.0600 x 0.0100/0.95e-6 = 631.6; Tu = 11.8/6.00 = 1.967; exponent 0.6967; Re^0.6967 = 89.32; Sc = 1727, Sc^(1/3) = 11.998; Sh = 0.664 x 89.32 x 11.998 = 711.6; k_L = 711.6 x 5.5e-10/0.0100 = 3.914e-5 m/s; I = 96485 x 2.00e-4 x 3.914e-5 x 100 = 0.0755 A. Sh/Sc^(1/3) = 59.3 lies among the measured points of the paper's Fig. 13 at this Re. Most tempting wrong answer, reached by a blind solver: the laminar flat-plate law with the resultant velocity sqrt(6.00^2 + 11.8^2) = 13.2 cm/s, Sh = 297, I = 32 mA (30 mA after rounding); with the mean velocity alone Sh = 0.664 x 25.13 x 11.998 = 200.2, I = 21 mA. The paper puts the turbulence into the exponent of Re, not into the velocity.
