Key by hand. B is 235 px right of A and 270 px above it: 357.95 px = 429.53 um, line at 48.96 degrees. K = 4 mu B/(mu + B) = 4 x 1.47 x 14.7/16.17 = 5.3455 nN/m. K a^2/(4 pi R) = 5.3455e-9 x 1.44e-10/(4 pi x 4.2953e-4) = 0.1426 fN. Angles of the Burgers vectors from the line: 11.04 and 251.04 degrees. Glide force on B from A = 0.1426 x cos(11.04) x cos(502.08) = 0.1426 x 0.9815 x (-0.7888) = -0.1104 fN along the Burgers vector of B. Glide force from the applied stress = tau a cos(2 x 300) = 3.50e-12 x 12.0e-6 x (-0.5) N = -0.0210 fN along the same vector (for a Burgers vector along +x with the extra half-rows above it is +tau a, the dislocation is driven to the right). Sum -0.1314 fN along the Burgers vector of B. That vector has the projection cos(251.04) = -0.325 on the line from A to B, so the force points along the glide line in the sense that leads away from A: +0.13 fN.

The most tempting wrong answer is +0.029 fN. It comes from taking both Burgers vectors, without reading them, along the horizontal rows with equal senses and the extra half-rows above: 0.1426 x cos(-48.96) x cos(-97.92) + 0.0420 = -0.0129 + 0.0420 = +0.0291 fN along a vector that has a positive part along the line. Opposite horizontal vectors give +0.055 or -0.029 fN, depending on which of the two is reversed. The part from dislocation A alone, with the applied stress left out, is +0.11 fN. With the right glide lines the four choices of the two senses give +0.13, -0.13, -0.089 and +0.089 fN. Right readings with other slips: applied part taken as tau a without cos(2 beta), 0.15 or 0.068; no factor 1/(1 - nu), 0.082; the two-dimensional Poisson ratio 0.82 in place of 0.45, 0.36; B in place of K, 0.32.

Check in a second (Python 3, standard library):
import math
mu, B, a, tau = 1.47e-9, 14.7e-9, 12.0e-6, 3.50e-12
dx, dy = 235, 270                                 # px, B minus A, y upwards
R, th = math.hypot(dx, dy)*120e-6/100, math.degrees(math.atan2(dy, dx))
K = 4*mu*B/(mu + B)
def F(bA, bB, t=tau):                             # directions of the two Burgers vectors, degrees
    pA, pB = math.radians(bA - th), math.radians(bB - th)
    g = K*a*a/(4*math.pi*R)*math.cos(pA)*math.cos(2*pB) + t*a*math.cos(math.radians(2*bB))
    return g*math.copysign(1, math.cos(pB))*1e15  # fN, positive away from A
print(F(60, 300), F(60, 300, 0), F(60, 120), F(240, 300), F(0, 0))

It prints 0.1314, 0.1104, -0.1314, -0.0894 and 0.0291.
