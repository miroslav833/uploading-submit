The figure is a simulated micrograph (594 x 257 px) and what is read from it is the sequence of facets of the junction line. The line runs from the left border to the right border. Its corners are at (x, y from the top, in px) (0, 199), (85, 114), (113, 143), (141, 114), (198, 171), (283, 86), (311, 114), (339, 86), (396, 143), (481, 58), (509, 86), (537, 58) and (594, 114). All facets are at 45 degrees. Their horizontal runs are 85, 28, 28, 57, 85, 28, 28, 57, 85, 28, 28 and 57 px, and they rise and fall in turn, beginning with a rise. In units of the shortest facet l the lengths are 3, 1, 1, 2, three times over: one period is four facets of 3 l, l, l and 2 l, the period length is 7 l, and the figure holds three periods.

Key by hand. The critical current density changes sign from each facet to the next (the d-wave lobes along a and b have opposite signs), so it is j0 sigma(x) with sigma = +1 on the rising facets and -1 on the falling ones, x the path length in units of l. The mean over a period is <sigma> = (3 - 1 + 1 - 2)/7 = 1/7. The phase is psi + xi(x), psi the mean over a period and xi a small part with the period of the line. The static phase equation (lambda_J/l)^2 phi'' = sigma sin(phi) gives xi' = (l/lambda_J)^2 v(x) sin(psi), where dv/dx = sigma - <sigma> and v has zero mean. The averaged supercurrent density is j0 [<sigma> sin(psi) + <sigma xi> cos(psi)] with <sigma xi> = -<v xi'> = -(l/lambda_J)^2 <v^2> sin(psi), so j1 = j0 <sigma> and j2 = -(1/2) j0 (l/lambda_J)^2 <v^2>. Here sigma - <sigma> is 6/7 on rising and -8/7 on falling facets, and v at the corners of one period is -10/7, 8/7, 0, 6/7, -10/7. On a facet of length L with v from p to q the integral of v^2 is L (p^2 + p q + q^2)/3: 84/49, 64/147, 12/49 and 152/147, in all 24/7, so <v^2> = 24/49. With lambda_J = 4 l: j1/j0 = 1/7 = 0.1429, j2/j0 = -(1/2)(1/16)(24/49) = -3/196 = -0.0153, and |j2/j1| = 3/28 = 0.107. A numerical solution of the full phase equation at lambda_J = 4 l, with no expansion, gives 0.1071.

The most tempting wrong answer is 0.214. The facets of each kind are then merged into one facet of 4 l and one of 3 l, for which <v^2> = 48/49, twice the true value. Leaving out the factor 1/2 of sin(psi) cos(psi) gives the same number. Other values: v started from zero at a corner with its mean not removed gives 0.554, the first power of l/lambda_J gives 0.429, j2/j0 in place of j2/j1 gives 0.0153, a factor 1/4 in place of 1/2 gives 0.0536.

Check in a few seconds (Python 3, standard library). The first number is the key, the second the most tempting wrong answer:
from fractions import Fraction as F
def ratio(L, lam=4):
    P = sum(L); s = [(-1)**k for k in range(len(L))]
    m = F(sum(a*b for a, b in zip(s, L)), P)
    v = [F(0)]
    for a, b in zip(s, L): v.append(v[-1] + (a - m)*b)
    vb = sum(b*(v[k] + v[k+1])/2 for k, b in enumerate(L))/P
    v = [q - vb for q in v]
    v2 = sum(b*(v[k]**2 + v[k]*v[k+1] + v[k+1]**2)/3 for k, b in enumerate(L))/P
    return float(v2/(2*lam**2)/m)
print(ratio([3, 1, 1, 2]))     # 0.10714
print(ratio([4, 3]))           # 0.21429
