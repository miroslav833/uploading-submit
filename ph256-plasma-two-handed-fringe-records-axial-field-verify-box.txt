Data the chart carries. Record B (the lower one): flat at a maximum until x = 93 px, then 116 maxima; the 10th, 20th, ... 110th at x = 192, 270, 353, 445, 541, 631, 718, 805, 887, 964, 1041 px and the 116th at 1126 px; the signal then falls and stays at +0.46 of the swing (maximum +1, minimum -1), which is arccos(0.46)/(2 pi) = 0.17 cycle: 116.17 cycles. Record A (the upper one): flat at a maximum until x = 191 px, then 98 maxima, the 10th to 90th at x = 281, 354, 434, 524, 612, 697, 782, 863, 940 px and the 98th at 1020 px; one more minimum follows, the signal rises and stays at +0.16 of the swing, 1 - arccos(0.16)/(2 pi) = 0.78 cycle: 98.78 cycles. The maxima are 7.6 to 9.6 px apart on B and 7.3 to 9.1 px apart on A over most of the chart, wider in the first and last few cycles. The two records have different time scales, so only the totals are used.

Key by hand. lambda = c/f = 1.0519 mm, L/lambda = 228.16. For waves along the field mu^2 = 1 - X/(1 -+ Y), X = (plasma frequency/wave frequency)^2, Y = cyclotron frequency/wave frequency (below one, as the question states), and each record shows N = (L/lambda)(1 - mu) cycles. 1 - mu = 0.5092 (B) and 0.4329 (A), mu = 0.4908 and 0.5671, X/(1-Y) = 0.7591, X/(1+Y) = 0.6784, Y = (0.7591 - 0.6784)/(0.7591 + 0.6784) = 0.05609, X = 0.7165. B = Y * 2 pi f/(e/m) = 0.05609 * 2 pi * 285.0e9/1.7588e11 = 0.5711 T, key 0.57 T.

Most tempting wrong answer. With the tenuous-plasma formulas (phase shift proportional to density, Faraday rotation to density times field) Y = (116.17 - 98.78)/(116.17 + 98.78) = 0.0809 and B = 0.82 T. The density is 72 per cent of cut-off, so these do not hold. Whole cycles only (116, 98) give 0.60 T.

Check program (Python, standard library):
import math
c, em, f, L = 2.9979e8, 1.7588e11, 285.0e9, 0.240
lam = c/f
u1, u2 = 116.17*lam/L, 98.78*lam/L
a1, a2 = u1*(2-u1), u2*(2-u2)
Y = (a1-a2)/(a1+a2)
print(Y, Y*2*math.pi*f/em)   # 0.0561, 0.571 T
