The answer is not taken from a paper. It follows from the givens and from the number of waveguides in the photograph, so the photograph's content is listed here as text.

What the photograph shows: a copper face with three rows of dark openings, 86 in each row, seen from one side. The spacing of the openings on the picture falls smoothly from about 8 px at the left end to about 5 px at the right end. Distances of the openings from the left edge in px on the line through the middle row, every tenth from the 10th to the 80th: 106, 182, 254, 323, 388, 449, 506, 561. The first is at 34 px and the 86th at 592 px. The verification notes list all 86 positions as recovered from the image file.

The key by hand. 86 waveguides in 0.8014 m give a period of 9.319 mm. A phase step of 90 degrees per period puts the main lobe at N_par = lambda_0/(4 p) = 59.958/(4 x 9.319) = 1.6086. In a cold plasma with W_ci << w << W_ce the slow and the fast root of the dispersion relation coincide where N_par = x + sqrt(1 + (1 - g) x^2), with x = w_pe/W_ce and g = W_ce W_ci/w^2 = (148.36 GHz x 40.42 MHz)/(5 GHz)^2 = 0.2399. Solving, x = [N_par - sqrt(N_par^2 - g (N_par^2 - 1))]/g = 0.5131, and n_e = eps0 B^2 x^2/m_e = 7.19 x 10^19 m^-3. The discriminant of the full cold-plasma quartic vanishes at 7.22 x 10^19 m^-3. Both round to 7.2.

Wrong answers that come up easily. Leaving out the ion term (N_par = x + sqrt(1 + x^2)) gives 6.6, the most tempting one. A count of 85 gives 6.8 and a count of 87 gives 7.6. Protons in place of deuterons give 7.9.

A short check in Python (standard library):

import math
e, me, eps0, c = 1.602176634e-19, 9.1093837e-31, 8.8541878e-12, 299792458.0
f, B, width, n, ratio = 5.000e9, 5.30, 0.8014, 86, 3670.5
N = c/f/(4*width/n)
g = (e*B/me/(2*math.pi*f))**2/ratio
x = (N - math.sqrt(N*N - g*(N*N - 1)))/g
print(round(N, 4), round(x, 4), round(eps0*B*B*x*x/me/1e19, 2))

It prints 1.6086 0.5131 7.19.

Sources for the method: Brambilla, Nucl. Fusion 16, 47 (1976); Bonoli, IEEE Trans. Plasma Sci. 12, 95 (1984).
