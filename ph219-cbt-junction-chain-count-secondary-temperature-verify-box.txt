That is 132 islands, and with one junction between neighbours and one at each pad, 133 junctions.

By hand. A junction of a uniform chain of N junctions sees the capacitance N C/(N - 1) between its electrodes, so one tunnelling event costs E = (N - 1) e^2/(2 N C) on top of the work of the source. At high bias every event goes forward at a rate linear in the voltage across the junction, which gives I R = <v> - E/e for each junction and V = N R I + (N - 1) e/(2C) for the chain: the offset 0.8052 mV equals (N - 1) e/(2C) at any temperature. At zero bias the cost of an event in units of kT is Gaussian with mean u/2 and variance u, u = (N - 1) e^2/(N C k T), and the conductance is the average of x/(e^x - 1), which is 1 - u/6 + u^2/60 - u^3/630. With u = a/T and a = 2 e V_off/(N k) = 0.14051 K, the dip 0.0517 = u/6 - u^2/60 + u^3/630 gives u = 0.3201 and T = 0.14051/0.3201 = 0.439 K, so 0.44 K. With 132 or 134 junctions T is 0.442 or 0.436 K, the same answer.

The most tempting wrong answer is 0.45 K. It keeps only the first-order depth u/6 of the dip, so that T = e V_off/(3 N k x 0.0517) = 0.453 K. The second-order term lowers the result by 3 per cent.

A check in Python, standard library only.

import math
k, e, Voff, dip, N = 1.380649e-23, 1.602177e-19, 0.8052e-3, 0.0517, 133
a = 2*e*Voff/(N*k)
u = 6*dip
for _ in range(30): u = 6*(dip + u*u/60 - u**3/630)
print(a, u, a/u)                      # 0.14051 K, 0.3201, 0.4389 K
print(e*Voff/(3*N*k*dip))             # 0.4530 K, first order only
