Section 2 of the attached notes prints it on the line "t = 1.9554e+09 s = 62.006 years -> 62 years", and Section 3 reaches it again on the line "INDEPENDENT RESULT: 62 years" by computing the chloride content at the bar forward in time and bisecting on the time of crossing. Section 4 holds the article's migration coefficients, assumptions and printed service lives as transcribed. By hand: the article's mixture of water-to-cement ratio 0.48 has a measured migration coefficient of 5.65e-12 m2/s, and its service-life estimates take D_ref = 0.5 D_ns = 2.825e-12 m2/s at 28 days with a threshold of 0.4 per cent by mass of cement. Then 1 - 0.4/1.8 = 0.777778 and its inverse error function is z = 0.86312, so D(t) t = (0.047/(2 x 0.86312))^2 = 7.41294e-04 m2. With D(t) = D_ref (t_ref/t)^m and m = 0.3, t^0.7 = 7.41294e-04/(2.825e-12 x 2419200^0.3) = 7.41294e-04/(2.825e-12 x 82.2435) = 3.19058e+06, so t = 1.9554e+09 s, which is 62.01 years of 365 days and 61.96 years of 365.25 days. The same calculation returns all 81 numerical service lives of the article's Tables 9 and 10, to the printed digit with a 365-day year, among them 35.0 years for this mixture at 35 mm and 1.5 per cent with the same aging factor. The tempting wrong answer is 10 years. It comes from a typical migration coefficient of 10e-12 m2/s for this water-to-cement ratio, used as the diffusion coefficient without conversion; the measured value is 5.65e-12 and the article halves it. Using the measured value without the halving gives 23 years, the usual default D28 = 10^(-12.06 + 2.4 w/c) = 1.24e-11 m2/s gives 8, averaging the diffusion coefficient over the exposure gives 37, and the neighbouring mixture of ratio 0.53 gives 64. Check program: import math; z=0.86312; print(math.erf(z), ((0.047/(2*z))**2/(0.5*5.65e-12*(28*86400)**0.3))**(1/0.7)/(365*86400)) prints 0.77778 and 62.007, the last digit reflecting the rounded z.
