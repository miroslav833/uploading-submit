Key: 81.2.D(b). It stands in the first section of the attached notes (output line KEY) and again in the second (line code).
By hand, from the page for bone 81 of the 2018 compendium (J Orthop Trauma 32 Suppl 1, section Foot): talus = 81; body = 1, neck = 2, head = 3, written between dots, and the fracture leaves the bone in front of the lateral process, so 81.2. Neck types: 81.2.A nondisplaced (Hawkins 1), 81.2.B displaced with subtalar subluxation or dislocation (Hawkins 2), 81.2.C displaced with talar body dislocation (Hawkins 3), 81.2.D displaced with talar body and head dislocation (Hawkins 4). Here the body is out of the ankle and of the subtalar joint and the head is out of the talonavicular joint, so D. Qualifications of B, C and D: a simple, b multifragmentary; three neck pieces give b, in round brackets. Universal modifiers are left out as the question says.
Most tempting wrong answers: 81.2.C(b), from counting the body dislocation and overlooking the head; 81.2C(d), from lettering the type by fragmentation as for a shaft and the Hawkins type as the qualification; 81.2D2 or 81B3, from the long-bone layout with a numbered group.
Check in seconds (Python, standard library):
body_out, subtalar_out, head_out, pieces = True, True, True, 3
t = 'D' if body_out and head_out else 'C' if body_out else 'B' if subtalar_out else 'A'
print('81.2.' + t + ('' if t == 'A' else '(b)' if pieces else '(a)'))   # 81.2.D(b)
