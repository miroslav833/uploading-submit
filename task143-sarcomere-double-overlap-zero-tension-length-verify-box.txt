Measured from the pixels of the figure (scale bar 480 px = 1 um), means over the three myofibrils: Z line to Z line 2.200 um, A band 1.600 um, pale central stripe 0.159 um, dense zone round it 0.504 um wide (six A bands, sd 0.012 um). The dense zone is 0.063 of full scale darker than the outer A band. That is the gray step of one more set of thin filaments: the I band, which holds one set, is 0.065 darker than the empty space between the myofibrils. So the dense zone holds thin filaments from both Z lines and its borders are their free ends. Thin filament = (2.200 + 0.504) / 2 = 1.352 um. Zero overlap at 1.600 + 2 x 1.352 = 4.30 um, key 4.3; a dense zone of 0.46 to 0.54 um gives 4.3 as well. The most tempting wrong answer takes the pale stripe for the H zone: thin = (2.200 - 0.159) / 2 = 1.02 um and 1.600 + 2.041 = 3.64 um, given as 3.6 or 3.7, the same as the frog values of the textbooks. It cannot be right, because an H zone is not bordered by a zone denser than the overlap region; 0.16 um is the bare zone of the thick filament. Where to look: any A band, 0.08 to 0.25 um on either side of the M line, best seen by averaging the gray level down the height of one myofibril. Check in Python: S, A, w, h = 2.200, 1.600, 0.504, 0.159; print(round(A + S + w, 2), round(A + S - h, 2)).
