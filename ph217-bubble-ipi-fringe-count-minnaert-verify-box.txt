A dark fringe meets the rim at both ends (rim at +-209 px), so the diameter holds N = 53 fringe periods.
Key by hand: m = 1.0003/1.3340 = 0.74985; reflection glare point b0 = a cos 22.5 deg = 0.92388 a; once-refracted ray with 2(r - i) = 45 deg and sin i = m sin r gives i = 43.046 deg, b1 = a sin i = 0.68258 a, on the same side as b0 for a bubble, separation kappa = 0.24130 a. The phase difference changes at k_w a kappa(theta) per radian with k_w = 2 pi n_w/lambda0; kappa falls from 0.2892 to 0.1991 across the aperture (the chirp), and its integral over the 0.1940 rad aperture is 0.046994. a = N lambda0/(n_w x 0.046994) = 0.44977 mm (0.45152 mm with kappa at 45 deg). Breathing mode: f = (1/2 pi a) sqrt([3 gamma (p0 + 2 sigma/a) - 2 sigma/a]/rho) = 7314.4 Hz (7286.0 Hz with the centre value), 7.3 kHz.
Most tempting wrong answer: the drop formula, glare points on opposite sides, separation cos 22.5 deg + sin i = 1.6065 a, gives a bubble 6.66 times smaller and 49 kHz. Vacuum wavelength gives 5.5 kHz, isothermal gas 6.2 kHz; 52 or 54 periods give 7.5 or 7.2 kHz.
Check (Python 3):
import math
m=1.0003/1.3340
def kap(t):
    h=t/2; i=math.atan(m*math.sin(h)/(1-m*math.cos(h))); return math.cos(h)-math.sin(i)
t0=math.radians(45)-0.097; I=sum(kap(t0+(j+0.5)*0.194/2000) for j in range(2000))*0.194/2000
a=53*532.0e-9/(1.3340*I); ps=2*0.0728/a
print(math.sqrt((3*1.4*(101.3e3+ps)-ps)/998.2)/(2*math.pi*a))  # 7314.4 Hz
