Key: 2.4 W (rotor power). In the attached notes it is the line 'KEY: P = 2.398 W' of the first script; the second script, forward integration with a secant search, prints 2.398 W. The correlation is Eq. (5) of Teshima and Ohashi, J. Chem. Eng. Japan 10, 70 (1977), p. 71: Sh = 2.0 + 0.012 (eps d_p^4/nu^3)^0.41 Sc^0.64, eps = P/(V rho_l) (Eq. 3), k_L = (V/At) ln(C0/C) (Eq. 2). By hand: k_L = 3.50e-3 x ln 10/(0.0403 x 600) = 3.333e-4 m/s; Sh = 169.4; nu = 8.052e-7 m2/s, Sc = 444.9, Sc^0.64 = 49.53; (Sh - 2)/(0.012 x 49.53) = 281.7 = G^0.41, so G = 9.439e5; eps = G nu^3/d_p^4 = 0.6879 W/kg; P = 0.6879 x 996 x 3.50e-3 = 2.40 W. The most tempting wrong answers come from correlations for suspended particles: Sano et al. (2 + 0.4 G^1/4 Sc^1/3) gives 23 W and Levins-Glastonbury 140 to 240 W. Check program (standard library): import math; nu=8.02e-4/996; Sh=3.5e-3*math.log(10)/(0.0403*600)*0.92e-3/1.81e-9; G=((Sh-2)/(0.012*(nu/1.81e-9)**0.64))**(1/0.41); print(G*nu**3/0.92e-3**4*996*3.5e-3)  prints 2.398.
