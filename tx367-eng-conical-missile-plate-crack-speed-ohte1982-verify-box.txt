Key: 105 m/s, 105.16 before rounding. Section 2 of the attached notes prints it on the line "v = sqrt(2 x 71884/13.0) = 105.16 m/s -> 105 m/s", and Section 3 reaches it again on the line "INDEPENDENT RESULT: 105 m/s (105.16 before rounding)" by bisection on the kinetic energy. Section 4 holds the paper's equations, units and conditions as transcribed. By hand: the paper gives E_f = 3.0e8 T^1.5 D_e^1.5 in kgf m with lengths in metres, D_e = T(1 + 2.9 tan(theta/2)^2.1) for a conical nose, and D_e = D_m only when that expression exceeds D_m. Here tan(20 deg) = 0.36397, its 2.1 power is 0.11974, and D_e = 1.3472 x 25.0 = 33.68 mm, below the 110 mm shank. Then T D_e = 8.4203e-04 m2, its 1.5 power is 2.4434e-05, E_f = 7330 kgf m = 71884 J, and v = sqrt(2 x 71884/13.0) = 105.16 m/s. The tempting wrong answer is 179 m/s. It comes from the BRL perforation formula with the shank diameter, E = 1.44e9 (0.110 x 0.025)^1.5 = 2.077e5 J; the paper's tests show that a conical nose cracks the plate at far lower energy than that. Putting the 110 mm shank into the paper's own formula gives 255 m/s, and taking the constant 3.0e8 to give joules gives 34 m/s. Check program: import math; k=1+2.9*math.tan(math.radians(20))**2.1; print(k, math.sqrt(2*9.80665*3.0e8*(0.025*0.025*k)**1.5/13.0)) prints 1.3472 and 105.16. Citation: Ohte and others, Bulletin of the JSME 25, 1226-1231 (1982), Eqs. (10) to (12) and conclusion (4).
