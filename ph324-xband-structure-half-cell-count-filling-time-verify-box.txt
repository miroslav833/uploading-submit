The answer is not taken from a paper. It follows from the givens and from the number of cells in the photograph, so the photograph's content is listed here as text.

What the photograph shows: one copper half seen from above on a dark cloth, a straight row of 67 dark cell pockets separated by thin bright iris ridges, the aperture groove running along the axis through every iris, a larger coupler pocket with a waveguide slot at each end and a short beam-pipe groove beyond it. The pockets are evenly spaced about 8.9 px apart. Cell centres in px from the left edge: 1st 86, 10th 166, 20th 254, 30th 344, 40th 434, 50th 522, 60th 612, 67th 674. The verification notes list all 67 as recovered from the image file.

The key by hand. d = 747.3/67 = 11.154 mm. Synchronism gives psi = omega d/c = 2.6705 rad (0.8501 pi). The operating point fixes kappa = (f^2/f0^2 - 1)/(1 - cos psi) = 0.03334. The group velocity is d dω/dψ = d (omega^2 - omega0^2)/(2 omega) cot(psi/2) = 5.698e6 m/s = 0.0190 c, and the front crosses the 747.3 mm in 131.1 ns. Answer 131 ns.

Wrong answers that come up easily. Counting the 68 irises as cells gives 122 ns, the most tempting one, and 66 cells give 142 ns. A standard phase advance of 5 pi/6 in place of the count gives 120 ns. The passband written linearly in omega gives 127 ns.

A short check in Python (standard library):

import math
c = 299792458.0
f, f0, L, n = 11.4240e9, 11.0800e9, 0.7473, 67
d = L/n
psi = 2*math.pi*f*d/c
vg = d*2*math.pi*(f*f - f0*f0)/(2*f)/math.tan(psi/2)
print(round(d*1000, 3), round(psi/math.pi, 4), round((f*f/f0**2 - 1)/(1 - math.cos(psi)), 5), round(vg/c, 4), round(L/vg*1e9, 2))

It prints 11.154 0.8501 0.03334 0.019 131.14.

Sources for the method: Nagle, Knapp and Knapp, Rev. Sci. Instrum. 38, 1583 (1967); Neal, J. Appl. Phys. 29, 1019 (1958).
