Key: 1.3 m/s, two significant figures. In the attached notes the first program prints "KEY: j_g = 1.3143 m/s -> 1.3 m/s at two figures", and the second finds 1.3076 m/s from the paper's other statement of the same transition (E_fg = 1.0 x 10^-3).

What the question leaves to the paper (Kataoka and Ishii 1984; NUREG/CR-3304, page 48, Eq. (99), repeated in the summary on page 58): the intermediate gas flux regime ends at j_g*/h* = 5.7 x 10^-4 N_mu_g^-0.5 D_H*^-0.42 (rho_g/drho)^0.10, "or at E_fg = 1.0 x 10^-3" (Eq. 100).

By hand: drho = 941.87 kg/m3; Laplace length 2.4398e-3 m; h* = 327.9; D_H* = 245.9; rho_g/drho = 1.1987e-3; N_mu_g = 1.27e-5/(1.129 x 0.0550 x 2.4398e-3)^0.5 = 1.0318e-3. Limit: 5.7e-4 x 31.132 x 0.09905 x 0.5104 = 8.970e-4. j_g* = 8.970e-4 x 327.9 = 0.2941. Velocity scale (0.0550 x 9.81 x 941.87/1.129^2)^0.25 = 4.4685 m/s, so j_g = 1.314 m/s. The outlet (h* = 327.9) is inside the momentum controlled region, which runs from h* = 21.1 to 437.7 at this flux.

Most tempting wrong answer: 8.4 m/s, from the limit taken as the plain number j_g*/h* = 5.7 x 10^-3 without the groups. Others: the density ratio to the power -0.23 in place of +0.10 in the limit, 12 m/s; the limit rebuilt as the point where the cubic law meets the near surface value, 21 m/s; the low to intermediate limit 6.39 x 10^-4, 0.94 m/s; 5.7 x 10^-4 without the groups, 0.84 m/s.
