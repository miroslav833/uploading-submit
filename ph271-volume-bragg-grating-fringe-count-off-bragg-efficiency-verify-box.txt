The answer is not taken from a paper. It follows from the givens, one count on the picture and Kogelnik's two-wave formulas, so the content of the picture is listed here as text.

What the picture shows. The image is 866 x 208 px; the micrograph fills rows 8 to 155. Upright grey fringes of sinusoidal profile cross it and continue past both ends of the scale. The scale below has two graduations, at x = 31 px (printed reading 0) and x = 829 px (printed reading 449.80 um). Adding the rows gives a profile with bright fringes at 31.0 px and 829.0 px under the graduations and 157 periods from one to the other. Every tenth bright fringe is at x = 31, 82, 133, 183, 234, 285, 336, 387, 438, 488, 539, 590, 641, 692, 743, 793, and the last at 829. The mean spacing is 5.083 px, and the Fourier transform of the profile between the graduations has its strongest period at 5.083 px (157.0 periods).

The key by hand. Period 449.80 um / 157 = 2.8650 um. In the glass the beam travels at asin(sin 10.487 deg / 1.4900) = 7.017 deg; the Bragg angle is asin(1.0640/(2 x 1.4900 x 2.8650)) = 7.159 deg (10.701 deg in air). Coupling strength nu = pi n1 d/(lambda cos theta) = 1.9337 with d = 1250 um. Dephasing xi = pi d (sin theta - sin theta_B)/(period x cos theta) = -3.410. Efficiency = sin^2(sqrt(nu^2 + xi^2))/(1 + xi^2/nu^2) = sin^2(3.920)/4.110 = 0.1200, that is 12 %. Moving any one given by half a unit of its last digit leaves 11.87 to 12.12 %.

Rigorous check. The notes solve the slab with index n + n1 cos(2 pi x/period) for all eight propagating orders, reflected waves included: 12.03 % in the first diffracted order, 87.97 % in the undiffracted beam, 0.001 % in all other orders, power conserved to eight digits.

Wrong answers that come up easily. 87 %: the beam taken to be on the Bragg angle (sin^2 nu). 18 %: the top of the first side lobe assumed. 2.3 %: weak-coupling formula nu^2 sin^2(xi)/xi^2. 6.5 %: no refraction at the face. Other values between 0 and 100 %: the fringe spacing judged by eye instead of counted.

Check (Python, standard library):
from math import *
per, n, lam, d, n1 = 449.80/157, 1.4900, 1.0640, 1250.0, 5.200e-4
s = sin(radians(10.487))/n; c = sqrt(1 - s*s)
nu = pi*n1*d/(lam*c); xi = pi*d*(s - lam/(2*n*per))/(per*c)
print(sin(sqrt(nu*nu + xi*xi))**2/(1 + xi*xi/(nu*nu)))    # 0.1200

Sources for the method: Kogelnik, Bell Syst. Tech. J. 48, 2909 (1969); Moharam and Gaylord, J. Opt. Soc. Am. 71, 811 (1981); Efimov et al., Appl. Opt. 38, 619 (1999); Ciapurin, Glebov and Smirnov, Opt. Eng. 45, 015802 (2006).
